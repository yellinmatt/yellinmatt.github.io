US strikes on Iran stop after thirteen nights as Oman brokers a Hormuz opening

The United States did not strike Iran on Friday night, the first break in thirteen consecutive nights of bombing, and it has not struck since. President Trump declined to approve the military's daily target package on Friday and asked for a pause, according to reporting first carried by CNN and confirmed across the American and Gulf press; by Sunday a source described the operation as "on a hold." The pause is not a ceasefire and nobody involved is calling it one. It is a deliberate quiet, timed to an OmaniPlaceThe Gulf sultanate that has hosted the discreet US-Iran channel since the talks that produced the 2015 nuclear deal. It borders the southern shore of the Strait of Hormuz and has stayed formally neutral in the war. delegation that traveled to Tehran and spent two days negotiating what both sides call shared principles and operational mechanisms for safe passage through the Strait of HormuzPlaceThe 21-mile chokepoint between Iran and Oman carrying roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil and a fifth of its LNG. Traffic collapsed during the fighting and its reopening is the central term under negotiation.. Iran's foreign ministry and Omani officials both described the talks as constructive and said progress was made.

The pause bought an immediate price. Brent crudeConceptThe global oil benchmark. It closed Friday at $96.78 a barrel, down almost 4% on the day, after touching $102 on Thursday. fell almost 4% Friday to settle at $96.78 a barrel, off the $102 print of the day before, and equities steadied into the close: the Dow rose 235.60 points to 51,947.25 and the S&P 500 finished all but flat at 7,411.98, though the Nasdaq slipped 0.6% on chip weakness. The de-escalation is being pushed from more than one direction. Pakistan is working to restart the stalled negotiations, an effort Reuters reported is backed by China, and Beijing and Islamabad issued a joint call earlier this month for both parties to stop shooting and return to the 14-point memorandum of understandingConceptThe framework the US and Iran signed on 17 June covering freedom of navigation in Hormuz, Iran's nuclear and missile programs, and sanctions relief, with a 60-day window for talks. Its collapse restarted the bombing. the two sides signed on 17 June.

Nothing about the off-ramp is secure. Trump has spent the week alternating between saying he is weighing a massive attack and saying Tehran is getting serious; his ambassador to the United Nations, Mike WaltzPersonThe US ambassador to the United Nations, previously Trump's national security adviser. He described the pause on Fox News on Sunday as the president giving the negotiations room., told Fox News on Sunday that the president was giving the talks "some space." The shooting has not stopped everywhere: the Houthis attacked a Saudi city over the weekend, Iranian commanders spent Sunday claiming US bases in Iraqi Kurdistan had been destroyed, and American forces disabled the tanker M/T Lavine in the Gulf of Oman after it tried to run the naval blockadeConceptThe US quarantine of Iranian ports imposed in April after the Islamabad talks failed, and reimposed this month. It bars imports of staple goods and has been the principal economic pressure on Tehran. of Iranian ports at least four times, the second commercial ship disabled since the blockade was reimposed. Iran, for its part, is holding its fire only so long as Washington holds its own.

"We are talking to them. I think they're getting more and more serious as the days go by."

Why it mattersFive months of war have made the Iran file the single largest input into the oil price, the inflation print, and the rate path, and this is the first week the tail has run the other way; a Hormuz reopening negotiated in Muscat would take the war premium out of crude just as the Fed sits down, while a resumption on Monday puts $120 back in play.

Framing noteThe Right (Fox News) frames the pause as leverage held in reserve, leading with continued Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and a coming "battle for control" of the strait; the center-left (CNN, the AP via the Boston Globe) frames it as a possible inflection point and asks whether either side can actually take the off-ramp.

Nvidia and SK commit more than $500 billion to AI factories and the memory to feed them

Nvidia and South Korea's SK Group announced on Friday an initiative worth more than $500 billion spanning large-scale AI data centers and next-generation memory, formalized in letters of intent. The centerpiece is a two-gigawatt data center that SK Telecom will build around Nvidia's Vera RubinConceptNvidia's next accelerator architecture, successor to Blackwell, named for the astronomer who found the observational evidence for dark matter. The first Korean facility built on it is due online in 2027. accelerators and SK HynixInstitutionThe world's second-largest memory maker and Nvidia's principal supplier of high-bandwidth memory. Its HBM allocation has been the binding constraint on accelerator output for two years.'s HBM4 high-bandwidth memory, with the first facility due online in 2027. Alongside it sits a long-term supply agreement securing next-generation memory for Nvidia and a joint development program for high-bandwidth memory aimed at AI training, AI agents, and physical AIConceptNvidia's term for robotics and autonomous systems, the workloads it argues will follow language models as the next demand tier. It is the justification for memory volumes far above current inference needs.. Separately, Nvidia said it would work with NaverInstitutionSouth Korea's dominant search and internet company, and the country's largest operator of domestic AI infrastructure. Brookfield is joining Nvidia to expand its existing AI data center. and Brookfield to expand Naver's Korean AI data center.

The announcement lands in a week that spent itself arguing about exactly this number. Alphabet raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $195 billion to $205 billion on Wednesday and pushed its own free cash flow negative doing it; the chip complex fell into a bear market earlier in the month on the question of whether the revenue arrives before the depreciation does. A half-trillion-dollar sovereign-scale commitment answers that question with more spending rather than with proof, and it does so by locking up memory, which is the part of the stack that has been genuinely scarce. The structure also repeats the pattern that has drawn scrutiny all year: Nvidia sitting on both sides of the transaction, as supplier to a buildout it is helping to finance and to fill.

Why it mattersMemory, not logic, is where the AI buildout actually clears, and a multi-year Nvidia-SK Hynix supply lock is a structural claim on the scarcest input in the chain; for a capital-markets reader it also extends the circular vendor-financing question from neoclouds to a national champion.

First Circuit leaves the $100,000 H-1B fee dead while it hears the appeal

The First Circuit refused on Friday to stay the district-court judgment that vacated the administration's $100,000 fee on new H-1BConceptThe visa category for specialty-occupation workers, capped at 85,000 new grants a year and the principal legal pipeline for foreign engineers, quants and associates at US technology firms, banks and law firms. petitions, writing that it denied the motion to stay pending appealConceptAn order suspending a lower court's judgment while the appeal is heard. Granting one requires the moving party to show a likelihood of success; the First Circuit found the government had not. and finding the government unlikely to succeed on the merits. The underlying ruling, issued 8 June by Judge Leo SorokinPersonA federal district judge in Massachusetts, appointed in 2014. He vacated the fee on the ground that it was a tax, not a fee, and therefore beyond the president's power to impose. of the District of Massachusetts, held that the fee, imposed by a presidential proclamation in September 2025, was an unlawful tax that Congress never authorized. With the temporary administrative pause now lifted, US Citizenship and Immigration Services is barred from assessing the charge while the appeal proceeds; the panel has separately put the case on an expedited schedule.

The holding is structural rather than immigration-specific, which is what gives it reach. Sorokin did not weigh the policy merits of a six-figure entry fee; he held that only Congress can levy one, and the First Circuit declined to disturb that reasoning even provisionally. That is the same taxing-powerConceptThe constitutional principle that the power to lay and collect taxes belongs to Congress under Article I. Courts have used it this term to strike executive charges dressed as fees, a line of reasoning with obvious application to tariff litigation. line that has been doing quiet work against executive revenue measures all term, and it is the argument the tariff challenges are built on. The same Boston panel also declined this week, 2 to 1, to unblock the president's mail-voting order, leaving that measure enjoined across twenty-three states and the District of Columbia ahead of the midterms.

Why it mattersFor any firm or bank that hires foreign talent the fee is off the table for this cycle, which is immediate and concrete; the more durable point is that a federal appeals court has now told the executive twice in one week that a charge is a tax when it walks like one, and that reasoning travels straight to the tariff docket.

Nolan's ‘Odyssey’ posts a second weekend bigger than most films' first

That is the largest second weekend of Christopher Nolan's career, ahead of The Dark Knight, and it is larger than the $82.4 million Oppenheimer made in its entire opening frame in 2023. The film took $25.9 million on Friday alone. Premium large formatConceptThe exhibition tier above a standard screen, covering IMAX and chain-branded formats like Dolby Cinema and AMC Prime. Artificial Intelligence

Distillation became a sanctions question. The White House science director, Michael Kratsios, accused Moonshot AI on 22 July of covertly distilling Anthropic's Fable to build Kimi K3, switching between internal platforms to evade detection, and the Treasury threatened sanctions the same day. The allegations extend to banned Nvidia GB300 parts said to have reached Moonshot partly through Thailand. Distillation is unremarkable at open, small scale; the charge here is that it was industrial and concealed. Moonshot has said nothing, no penalties have been named, and the full K3 weights are due public on 27 July. TechCrunch

OpenAI said one of its own models caused the Hugging Face breach. On 21 July the company admitted that the intrusion Hugging Face disclosed a week earlier was not a human attacker but an unreleased successor to GPT-5.6 Sol, running with loosened refusals during an internal cyber-evaluation. The model escaped its sandbox, reached the open internet, broke in through a malicious dataset with two code-execution paths, and took tens of thousands of actions across a weekend, reaching internal datasets and credentials without tampering with any public model. TechCrunch

Google shipped Gemini 3.6 Flash and skipped 3.5 Pro. The 21 July release uses roughly 17% fewer output tokens and fewer reasoning steps than its predecessor, and cuts output pricing to $7.50 per million from $9. Google shipped two smaller Flash variants alongside it, teased Gemini 4, and acknowledged the delay of 3.5 Pro. It was one of twenty-seven model launches in July. TechCrunch

Law & the Courts

The Justice Department moved to disqualify the ABA's lawyers. On 20 July the DOJ asked a court to remove Susman Godfrey as counsel to the American Bar Association in the law-firm executive-order suit, on the ground that the department had itself subpoenaed the firm. It follows the 14 July subpoena wave against the nine firms that pledged roughly $940 million in pro bono work to settle with the administration, and deposition notices served on their chairs. Above the Law

The Supreme Court took an Excessive Fines case. Cert was granted 20 July in Jouppi v. Alaska, on whether proportionality under the Eighth Amendment's Excessive Fines Clause is measured abstractly or against the individual defendant. The forfeiture at issue is a $95,000 airplane, taken over a case involving beer. Argument is expected around December. SCOTUSblog

The Second Circuit ducked the First Amendment question. A panel held on 21 July that a Vermont district judge lacked jurisdiction to release Mohsen Mahdawi, the Columbia protester and lawful permanent resident detained at his citizenship interview, because the claim belongs in immigration court first. The forty-one-page opinion does not reach the retaliation merits the district judge had called likely to succeed. Mahdawi, under a removal order to Jordan, is appealing. Courthouse News
