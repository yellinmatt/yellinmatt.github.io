The Splash · AI · November 21

Lean’s keepers certify a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, posted by an Anthropic mathematician

The community that maintains the proof assistant said on Saturday that it has “full confidence” in a machine-checked proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, posted on Friday. Alpöge’s four-page note opens: “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.”

The formal proof runs to about 17 million lines of Lean and covers the ; attempts at a human-readable version ran past 250,000 pages. The joint statement, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Initiative, says the theorem matches the library’s own definition, that dozens of people reproduced the same build and that independent checkers agreed. Alpöge told Quanta he had the result on Monday and waited; he called the timing, the day after Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut, “horrible.” Anthropic has posted no statement.

Number theorists split on what has been shown. James Maynard of Oxford told IBM Think it would be “clearly a historic milestone in mathematics” if it holds. Maksym Radziwiłł of NYU told Quanta the note contains “nonsensical statements” and that the formal proof does not explain why the result is true. wrote that the approach draws on the Langlands program. A separate 940-page human proof of the generalized hypothesis by Alessandro Masullo also surfaced Friday, with no machine check.

We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct.
Lean community and Mathlib joint statement, November 21

Why it mattersThe most famous open problem in mathematics may have fallen to a commercial model, with a certificate no human can read in full. Verification without understanding is now a working question for every profession that signs off on work, law included.

Primary: Lean community statement ↗
Also reported by: The Guardian via AOL, Quanta Magazine, IBM Think, Terence Tao

Go deeper
  • Alpöge posted the claim on Friday, November 20, the day after Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq.
  • The Lean community and Mathlib certified the statement and the proof on Saturday.
  • Anthropic has issued no statement, and its newsroom carries no post on the proof.

Background. Bernhard Riemann stated the conjecture in 1859: every nontrivial zero of the zeta function lies on the line with real part one half. It is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize problems, which pay $1 million only after publication in a qualifying outlet, a two-year wait and general acceptance by the field. Formal checking has grown with Mathlib, a community library that lets a proof be tested against a definition the prover did not write. Earlier AI-assisted work had placed at least 67.25% of the relevant zeros on the critical line, IBM Think reported. Simon Willison flagged both Friday notes, Alpöge’s and Masullo’s, within hours of posting.

What’s next. Anthropic’s shares trade again on Monday, November 23, the first session since the claim and its certification.
