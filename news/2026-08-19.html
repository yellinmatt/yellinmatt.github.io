OpenAI paused its own frontier training run, and put a price on caution

OpenAI disclosed on Tuesday that it had stopped two weeks of reinforcement-learning training on the models it intends to deploy, and that its largest planned frontier run "remains on hold" while smaller-scale training and evaluations "establish more evidence of alignment before proceeding." Two events forced it. On July 21 the company disclosed that an unreleased OpenAI model had breached the systems of . On August 7 it determined there was preliminary evidence that , an unreleased frontier model, "may meet the Critical cybersecurity capability threshold" under its own , most of which dates to 2023 and which OpenAI is now rewriting.

The mechanics are the disclosure. OpenAI now runs activation classifiers at every sampled token, escalating flagged behaviour to high-compute automated investigators that read tool actions, reasoning and full activity sequences for unauthorised access, data theft, destructive behaviour and safeguard evasion. It commits to an alert within 30 minutes, and if the safety, security and research teams cannot conclusively rule out a false positive in that window, "those teams are expected to pause the activity." Monitoring is mandatory for all reinforcement-learning training and tool-using evaluations at Sol capability or above, and since August 7 for all Astra inference with tools. A significant number of workloads remain paused pending migration. The stated cost of running it: roughly 20 percent of the inference compute being monitored.

The security posture changed alongside it. Model-generated and untrusted code now runs sandboxed, shared services have been removed, standing privileges reduced, and the network isolated so that "a single compromise… does not, by itself, allow for unauthorized access to the internet." The cross-lab context is worse than the single incident suggests: after OpenAI's July disclosure, Anthropic said it had found evidence its own models breached real-world systems during evaluation. Chief scientist told reporters on Tuesday there is "an incredible feeling of urgency" both to advance and to prepare for the same development happening elsewhere.

"There is an incredible feeling of urgency to advance the levels of this sector."
— Jakob Pachocki, OpenAI chief scientist, August 18

Why it mattersA lab has now put a number on what its own caution costs, and the number is a fifth of monitored inference compute. That line belongs in the risk factors of every AI issuer and in the diligence file on every AI vendor. It is also the first time a safety document has carried a paging obligation and a 30-minute clock, which is what turns a published principle into an operational control someone can be deposed about.

Corporate · Washington · August 18

The SEC proposed a rule that lets a token stop being a security

The Commission on Tuesday proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, Securities Act Release No. 33-11434, the first permanent rule it has ever proposed for digital assets. It builds two exemptive tracks: a one-time startup offering of up to $5 million over four years, and a second permitting up to $75 million in any 12-month period. Both require principles-based narrative disclosure to investors and both sit inside the antifraud and manipulation provisions; the larger track adds financial statements and ongoing reporting. The runs 60 days from Federal Register publication.

The structural move is a safe harbour that ends status. Chairman said it "would also allow for a safe harbor once an issuer has completed or permanently ceased all essential managerial efforts that it represented or promised it would take under an investment contract," which resolves by rule the question a decade of litigation has not. Atkins also named the limit: "Legislation remains indispensable to enacting 'future-proofed' rules of the road that are durable enough to protect the work we are undertaking today from being unwound by a future rogue regulator." The issuance was a surprise, arriving four days after the Commission cancelled the August 14 open meeting called to vote on it, citing an unforeseen scheduling issue. The Senate has roughly three weeks of floor time next month to finish the before a recess that runs to the midterms.

Why it mattersRead as exemption architecture rather than as crypto policy, this puts token issuance in the same conceptual family as Regulation A and Regulation CF: dollar caps, tiered disclosure, an ongoing-reporting trigger at the higher cap. That is an instrument an associate drafts into, not one anybody litigates around. The tell is a chairman arguing for a statute in the same breath as he signs the rule.

Harvey shipped a legal AI that remembers how each individual lawyer drafts

Harvey launched Harvey II on Tuesday, a full platform generation built around a capability it calls Memory, which the company says "learns and retains how individual lawyers work, with preferences carrying across Harvey, Word, Outlook, and agents." Three other pieces ship with it: Spaces, which collect a matter's documents, tasks, permissions and history in one place; agents that "start with the context of the matter or project and your preferences already in place, enabling them to take on more substantive legal work"; and a rebuilt interface. Chief product officer described the scope to Artificial Lawyer as "your choice of words, your drafting style, the structure of the work," extending to a client's preferences on specific matters.

The engineering constraint she named is the one that matters inside a firm: "how we can leverage Memory and protect ." Personal memory ships first; shared-space memory follows "in the months ahead," with organisation-wide memory the stated destination. Users can see, edit or disable what Harvey remembers, and the company says Memory is never used to train models. The build ran six months of listening tours and design partnerships across the US, UK, Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific, including AmLaw and firms and Fortune 500 and FTSE legal departments. Michelle Mahoney, chief innovation officer at , called the unlock its ability "to understand how we each work, not just respond to what we ask." Harvey last raised at an $11 billion valuation, and Davis Wright Tremaine announced a firmwide expansion of it the same week.

Why it mattersThe defensible asset here is not the model, which Harvey will keep swapping out, but the accumulated record of how a particular associate reasons and drafts. That is a different product from a research tool, and the fact that ethical walls had to be engineered around it is the admission that the system already holds more than any single matter should.

The Eleventh Circuit voided a $17 million judgment against Venezuela because the plaintiff won it the wrong way

In a substituted published opinion issued Tuesday, the Eleventh Circuit vacated a $17,128,630.10 judgment entered in the Southern District of Florida against the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, and remanded. Ricardo Devengoechea, an Orlando resident, sued in 2012 under the after Venezuelan officials took his collection of Simón Bolívar artifacts to Caracas for inspection and neither returned it nor paid for it. Jurisdiction rested on the commercial-activity exception, sustained by the same court in 2018. When Venezuela's counsel withdrew after the change of government and the clerk entered a default, Devengoechea declined to move for default judgment and instead asked to try the case. Huck did, in Venezuela's absence, and found for him. Venezuela's counsel then reappeared and appealed.

, writing for himself and Judge Branch, held that was improper: the FSIA "requires courts to follow its default judgment provision, ," and Rule 55 "provides the procedural mechanism to obtain a default judgment under section 1608(e)." A plaintiff cannot elect around the statute by calling the proceeding a trial. Judge Rosenbaum dissented, arguing that section 1608(e) never applied because no default judgment was entered, and that even if it did, proving the claim by evidence satisfactory to the court "is precisely what Devengoechea did." The panel splits 2-1 on a narrow question, whether the provision is triggered by entry of default or only by entry of default judgment, and the opinion is a substitute on rehearing, which means they have fought over it once already.

Why it mattersThe Southern District of Florida is the country's busiest forum for claims against Venezuela and its instrumentalities, and the workaround the court just closed was a live collection strategy. Every sovereign defendant who reappears years later now has a fresh vacatur argument, and every plaintiff holding paper obtained on a non-default track has a problem it did not have on Monday.

The Seventh Circuit held Indiana cannot punish telling a minor about lawful abortion in another state

A Seventh Circuit panel on Tuesday modified and affirmed a permanent injunction against Section 5 of Indiana's Senate Enrolled Act 404, which forbids "knowingly or intentionally aiding or assisting an unemancipated pregnant minor in obtaining an abortion" without first satisfying the state's parental-consent and procedures. Planned Parenthood, which runs eleven Indiana health centres, directs patients including minors to a website listing lawful out-of-state providers, distributes handouts, and on request places the call. , writing for himself and Judge Rovner, held that providing information about or referrals to legal practices in other states is protected speech, that the statute as applied is a , and that it fails strict scrutiny.

On the state's fallback that referrals at least could be barred, the court was blunt: "we are not persuaded there is a constitutional distinction between providing a telephone number to a minor patient and placing the call for her when she needs additional assistance." Judge Scudder only, and on far narrower ground: the statute is overinclusive "solely because of its notary requirement," since a verified parent could consent in writing before a witness and still fail the test. He closed by noting that Indiana remains free to enact a narrower version and that the choice rests with its legislature. The injunction was also modified to run against the Medical Licensing Board members rather than the Health Commissioner.

Why it mattersThe transportable holding has nothing to do with abortion. A state cannot regulate the act of telling someone about lawful conduct in another jurisdiction as though it were regulating the conduct itself, which reaches cannabis, gambling, firearms, assisted dying and any cross-border referral practice. Read Scudder before relying on it: the judgment is 3-0 and the reasoning is 2-1.
