The Splash · News · Jerusalem · November 7–8 · cross-spectrum LCR

Herzog hands Eisenkot, not Netanyahu, the first chance to form Israel’s next government

President Isaac Herzog on Saturday gave , the former army chief who leads the opposition alliance, the to form a government, after lawmakers holding 67 of the Knesset’s 120 seats recommended him and 46 backed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Al Jazeera and the Associated Press reported. The formal presentation was set for 5 p.m. Israeli time on Sunday, Fox News reported.

The 67 include a record 19 seats won by Arab-led parties in the October 27 election, among them , while Eisenkot’s own four-party alliance holds 48. Netanyahu’s bloc won 53, and the seven members of endorsed no one. Winning both ultra-Orthodox parties would give Eisenkot exactly 61, and Al Jazeera noted he could govern with fewer by surviving a .

Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14. If he fails, Herzog can name another candidate, a Knesset majority can then back a third, and only after that does a new election follow within 90 days, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker. Eisenkot wrote that “this is the time to establish a national emergency government.” Netanyahu called instead for a national unity government and warned against hastily swearing in one that relied on Arab votes. The Joint List’s Ahmad Tibi called the decision “expected.” The AP noted that coalition talks will turn on draft exemptions for the ultra-Orthodox and on a state inquiry into the October 7 failures.

Such a government, dependent on Islamists, will dismantle the state of Israel from within.
Benjamin Netanyahu, via Al Jazeera, November 7

Why it mattersEvery road to 61 now runs through the Arab parties or the draft fight with the ultra-Orthodox, and whoever wins it inherits the Iran war and the ceasefire diplomacy. Netanyahu keeps the office while the clock runs.

Framing noteThe left (Al Jazeera) led with the Arab parties’ record seats as the margin that carried the endorsement; the right (Fox News) framed the same arithmetic as “deep coalition divisions over Arab parties and conscription” that cloud his path.

AI / Markets · San Francisco · November 8 · single-tier coverage: C

Anthropic takes over a stranded $3.4 billion order for gas generators, the FT reports

Anthropic agreed in late September to pay $3.4 billion for large Caterpillar , about 1.7 gigawatts of capacity, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, calling it the biggest known single purchase of power equipment by an AI company. The had been placed about two years earlier by OnePWR Solutions, a San Antonio power supplier that could not finance them. Anthropic took over the orders and the installation work without buying the company.

Neither company commented, and where the units will run is not known. The disclosure lands as Anthropic prepares a listing whose amended filing showed $895 billion of commitments, mostly compute. Renaissance Capital said on Friday that the roadshow could launch as early as Tuesday, with pricing possible the week of Nov. 16. Conflict disclosed: Anthropic’s model helps produce this paper.

Why it mattersPower, not chips, is the constraint an IPO cannot finance away. Buying a stranded queue slot is how an issuer shows investors the build is real before it prices.

EASL / Law · San Francisco · November 6–8 · single-tier coverage: C

Elvis Presley’s rights holders and Eminem’s publisher sue Suno, the AI music generator

Eight Mile Style, which owns much of Eminem’s catalog, sued Suno late Friday in federal court in San Francisco, seeking of $150,000 for each of 269 songs plus $672,000 for stripped copyright information, about $41 million in all, Variety reported. Eminem is not a party. A separate 51-page complaint by Authentic Brands Group and Presley’s publishers adds a claim under Tennessee’s : “The next Elvis won’t pick up a guitar.”

The same four lawyers, among them, filed both. Suno says it bars artists’ names in prompts and screens for misuse. It raised $400 million in June at a $5.4 billion valuation and still faces suits from Universal and Sony, while Warner settled for a license in 2025. Conflict disclosed: the Presley publishers are separately suing Anthropic.

Why it mattersOne firm, two famous catalogs and a state publicity claim stacked on copyright make a template. The AI music settlements so far have been licenses, and these plaintiffs are setting their price.

Markets & Deals · Tokyo and Zurich · November 7–8 · single-tier coverage: C

Blocked by Beijing, SoftBank and ABB weigh cutting China out of their $5.4 billion robotics deal

SoftBank Group and are studying a carve-out that would put the robotics unit’s China business into a separately owned entity, Bloomberg reported on Saturday, after China’s blocked SoftBank’s purchase on Thursday. China is the unit’s largest market, with a major plant in Shanghai. ABB is also weighing other buyers, who could value the business at $2 billion to $3 billion.

SoftBank shares fell 11% in Tokyo on Friday, and ABB lost more than 5% on the week. Citi analyst Martin Wilkie said the block roughly halves ABB’s capacity for acquisitions, to about $5 billion. The unit employs about 7,000 people and had $2.3 billion of revenue in 2024.

Why it mattersBeijing has used its review to stop a Japanese buyer taking a Swiss asset. Any cross-border deal with a China footprint now has to price that veto, and a geographic carve-out is the precedent to watch.

Also reported by: Japan Times, Investing.com, Euronext / Reuters

News · Kraków · November 7 · cross-spectrum LCR

A gunman with far-right views kills six in Kraków’s Main Square, and three relatives are found dead at his home

A 25-year-old Polish man drove around Kraków’s on Saturday afternoon, stopping to fire an at each stop, killing six people, four women and two men, and wounding 15, the Associated Press reported. At least three of the dead were Polish and one was British. Police detained him and say he acted alone.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said “everything indicates that he was fascinated by far-right ideologies.” Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the bodies of three close relatives were found at the suspect’s home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 150 kilometers away. The defense minister called it an act of terror, and the city is in official mourning until Wednesday.

Why it mattersA far-right massacre in the country’s most visited square lands on a government already fighting the president over the courts, and the source of the weapon will decide which fight comes next.
