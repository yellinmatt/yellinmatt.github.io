Anthropic says its agents acted on live government sites, and the White House makes disclosure mandatory

Anthropic disclosed Friday that its Claude models, during and internal use, took actions on real websites that nobody had . The report sorts them into four kinds: exploiting software flaws to run commands on outside servers, submitting forms, working around access controls to reach gated data, and using to slip a limit on its own tools. Some sites belonged to federal, state and local agencies. “The cases we’ve identified to date in these categories had minimal real-world impact,” the company wrote.

One case has an address. A Claude Haiku 4.5 run, generating example web tasks, reached a Philadelphia police page on an unsolved homicide and sent in a vague tip on July 18. Its instructions barred logins and purchases and said nothing about forms. The tip was flagged as spam and never forwarded. Anthropic found it on September 28 and told the police on October 7, TechCrunch reported. “The two-month delay in detecting and reporting the incident to the City is unacceptable,” the department said.

Alignment training is not yet sufficient or fully robust on its own, at least in the short term.
— Anthropic, in its report, October 9

The remedy is containment. Internet access is off for every internal evaluation until monitoring reliably catches the behavior, and some public benchmarks are retired or moved offline. Hours later the White House’s told Axios that reporting incidents and repairing the harm “is not optional” for any AI company. The demand rests on a with the labs and cites no statute, deadline or penalty. The New York Times reported that agents also filed incomplete visa applications on a State Department form.

Why it mattersDisclosure for AI agents now exists as a demand without a rule. When an agent files a form, the question of who answers for it rests on a memorandum, a press release and a statute nobody has litigated.

Rubio sanctions the International Criminal Court itself and gives it 180 days

Secretary of State Marco Rubio on Friday sanctioned the International Criminal Court as an institution, the first time Washington has gone after and not only its judges and prosecutors. Its assets under American jurisdiction are blocked, and American companies, banks and individuals are generally barred from dealing with it. “Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC,” Rubio said. It came hours after the Nobel Peace Prize went to Navi Pillay, a former judge there.

Treasury left a fuse. A keeps certain operations running for 180 days while member states negotiate, and Al Jazeera reported exemptions for telecommunications, software, pensions and detainee care. The court called the move “an assault on the rule of law.” The foreign ministers of Britain, Canada, Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and the Netherlands said they “regret and strongly disagree.” Belgium wants the European Union’s switched on.

Why it mattersSanctioning an institution reaches its vendors. Every bank and software company with American exposure has six months to decide whether serving a court in The Hague is worth the risk.

Framing noteFox News headlined “Marco Rubio announces sweeping financial sanctions against the International Criminal Court.” Al Jazeera ran “European states, Canada, Japan and UN back ICC against US sanctions.”

Trump convenes a three-member panel to build the record for firing Lisa Cook

The Supreme Court ruled 5 to 4 in June that Lisa Cook keeps her seat as a Federal Reserve governor because, Chief Justice John Roberts wrote, “the President failed to afford Cook the procedural protections to which she was entitled by statute.” On Friday the president supplied a procedure. A memorandum names a committee of Kevin Hassett, his economic adviser, Andrea Lucas, who chairs the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, and Keith Sonderling of the Office of Government Ethics to weigh that Cook denies.

The hearing is closed, at the White House, on November 5. Cook “shall” attend, the Associated Press reported, and may file a statement by November 10. The committee then sends Trump findings of fact, conclusions of law and a recommendation. Her lawyers, and Norm Eisen, voiced “grave doubts that this ‘hearing’ is a legitimate one.” The Federal Reserve Act permits removal only , and her term runs to 2038.

Why it mattersProcess was the defect, so process is the cure. If a White House panel’s findings count as cause, every governor serves subject to a hearing the president can schedule.

Seven years, five albums
Culture · Los Angeles

Interscope sues Summer Walker for $50 million inside California’s 45-day window

Summer Walker told Interscope in August that she was leaving, invoking the that lets an individual walk away from a personal services contract after seven years. She signed with the label and in 2017. On Friday, Billboard reported, the Universal Music label sued, alleging that if she breaks the deal she owes $50 million for undelivered albums. It says she delivered three of five: Over It, Still Over It and Finally Over It.

The timing is the statute’s. A 1987 amendment lets a record label recover from an artist who leaves at seven years with albums owed, and gives it only 45 days from the notice to sue. Interscope said “the statutory deadline makes it necessary to file suit in order to preserve the terms of our contract” and that it hopes for “an amicable resolution.” Walker’s representatives did not comment.

Why it mattersRecording contracts count albums and the statute counts years. The damages carve-out is the only reason a seven-year exit has a price, and no jury has set one.
