The Splash · Corporate / AI · New York and San Francisco · August 21

Anthropic's bankers are telling investors it could raise more than $100 billion, and the prospectus is expected to name the backlash against AI as a risk

Anthropic's bankers have told potential investors the company could seek to raise more than $100 billion at a valuation of up to $2 trillion, the New York Times reported on Friday evening, a figure that would make it the largest initial public offering ever conducted. Wire summaries of the Times report put the current record at SpaceX's June listing, which raised $85.7 billion at a $1.77 trillion valuation, and Anthropic's last private mark at $965 billion in June, meaning the company is asking the public market to roughly double a valuation set nine weeks earlier. Anthropic filed confidentially in June; the public prospectus is expected within weeks and could land as soon as the end of this month. The company is five years old, reported an annualized revenue run rate of roughly $65 billion at the end of July, and would join Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia as one of a handful of firms ever to carry a $2 trillion mark.

CNBC reported the same evening that chief financial officer has spent the month in meetings in San Francisco, answering questions on competition, on margin pressure from open-source models, and on a slowdown in data-centre construction. The detail that matters is what the filing will say rather than what it will ask for: people familiar with the process told CNBC the prospectus is expected to list public opposition to artificial intelligence, and to data centres specifically, as a key , because compute capacity correlates directly with revenue. A Gallup survey published in May found seven in ten Americans oppose data-centre construction near them.

That risk factor is not boilerplate, and the week supplied the proof. The Wall Street Journal reported Friday that Greg Abbott and Josh Shapiro, governors who between them courted roughly $60 billion of announced data-centre investment, have both moved to slow it. Abbott stood beside Sundar Pichai in Midlothian, Texas, as Google announced $40 billion of state investment and called Texas "the epicenter of AI development," then, less than nine months later, paused the advancement of some large data-centre loads through part of 's grid-connection queue. Shapiro, who appeared with Amazon in 2025 to announce $20 billion of Pennsylvania data centres, signed Executive Order 2026-05 in Harrisburg on August 18 describing developers who push speculative proposals without local support as predatory, removing AI data centres from , prohibiting nondisclosure agreements on data-centre projects, and conditioning environmental permits on a binding local commitment.

"The epicenter of AI development."
— Greg Abbott on Texas, quoted in The Wall Street Journal, August 21

Why it mattersA $100 billion raise is not a bigger version of an ordinary deal, it is a different animal: no syndicate has ever allocated that much paper at once, and the book-building, stabilisation and lock-up mechanics will be improvised in public by the same capital-markets groups an incoming associate joins. Read the risk factors before the financials. A registrant conceding that permitting politics can throttle its revenue is telling you where the litigation and the deal work will actually be.

News · Tehran, Paris and the Gulf · August 21–22 · cross-spectrum LLLC

Iran threatens the pipelines that bypass Hormuz on the same weekend Macron and the Saudi crown prince meet in Paris to build them

, named this month as secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said in an interview aired on Iranian state television on Saturday that Iran would strike the oil-export routes that serve as alternatives to the Strait of Hormuz if neighbouring states join the American economic campaign. Those neighbours would be treated as enemies, he said, and "we will target their interests." On the American president: "If (Trump) wants to do something, we will retaliate in a seismic manner." The threat has a specific object. The are the only physical answer to a closed strait, and on Friday and Saturday Emmanuel Macron and Mohammed bin Salman convened the inaugural in Paris to discuss exactly that: alternative pipelines, regional rail, and maritime routes around the chokepoint.

Tehran spent the same weekend demonstrating that the strait is a valve it controls rather than a door it has shut. Iranian state news agency IRNA said Iran had granted a number of Iraqi tankers special permission to transit, following repeated requests from Baghdad and, by IRNA's account, one of the main asks during parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf's visit to Iraq last week. Iraq has no national tanker fleet, which is what makes selective passage worth granting. Ghalibaf posted on Saturday that Iran had received "numerous messages" from neighbouring countries about "reshaping new security arrangements," adding that Washington "put the security of every single one of its allies at such risk" that they "briefly saw their entire existence on the line." Independent counts of the waterway remain grim: logged 21 full inbound and 18 outbound transits in the week ending August 7, roughly four percent of the pre-conflict average, and still rates mariner risk in the strait as severe.

Why it mattersThreatening the bypass lines is an escalation with no military content and enormous commercial content: it converts Saudi and Emirati infrastructure from a hedge into a target, which reprices every barrel that was supposed to be safe. Watch whether marine underwriters extend the Hormuz war-risk premium westward to the Red Sea and Fujairah terminals. That, not the rhetoric, is where the threat becomes a number.

Framing noteThree incompatible pictures of the same waterway circulated inside 48 hours. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said on Friday that "thanks to the U.S. Navy, oil is flowing through the Strait of Hormuz," citing an average eight million barrels a day over seven days. CBS News, reading the UKMTO figures the same day, put traffic at "only about four percent of the pre-conflict average." CNN's own read of UKMTO data put ship numbers at twenty percent of the pre-war average. The three measure different vessel classes over different weeks and should not be averaged.

Law · Washington · August 21

The Education Department moves to strip the ABA of its power to accredit law schools, on the same day the ABA gutted three of its own diversity standards

The Department of Education is recommending that the lose its federal recognition as a law school accreditor, a role the association has held since 1952 across what are now 198 accredited schools. The supporting memorandum runs nearly 500 pages and argues that the Council is not sufficiently independent of the profession to focus on educational quality; as of Friday afternoon the report itself had not been released publicly. "We have said many times that accreditation, in general, operates like a cartel," assistant secretary David Barker told the Wall Street Journal, which obtained an advance copy. "When there's no competition, accreditors feel free to inject their own political preferences, their own ideologies into the work that they do." The recommendation now goes to and then to Undersecretary Nicholas Kent; a Federal Register notice set the hearing for September 23 and 24, where the Council's immediate past chair, Daniel Thies, is scheduled to appear. Council chair said the Council "is confident it complies with the Higher Education Act."

The consequence is narrower than the headline, and worth holding onto. Accreditation for student-loan eligibility is legally distinct from accreditation for bar licensure, as Professor Derek Muller pointed out the same day; most law schools would fall back on their parent university's accreditor, and by his estimate only two to four standalone schools would need to find a new one. The timing is the other half of the story. Hours before the recommendation surfaced, the same Council voted to eliminate or narrow three of its diversity requirements, including the bias and cross-cultural competency education requirement and its nondiscrimination language, with two of the three still requiring a separate ABA vote and a decision on itself due September 8. Trump's April 2025 executive order on accreditation had directed Education Secretary Linda McMahon to assess suspending or terminating the Council, citing precisely those requirements. NACIQI found 17 ABA compliance failures in 2011 and recommended a one-year suspension in 2016, which the Department declined to impose.

Why it mattersThe accreditor is the least visible and most load-bearing institution in a law student's life: it sets the credit hours, the clinical requirements, the employment-outcome disclosures, and, through the state supreme courts that defer to it, the path to a bar card. Losing federal recognition would not close a school, but it would end the single national standard and hand the question to fifty jurisdictions, which is how a portable degree stops being portable.

Corporate · AI · Santa Clara · August 22

Nvidia tells its biggest customers that AI server prices are rising more than 15 percent, four days before it reports

Some of Nvidia's largest customers have been notified that server prices will rise by more than 15 percent in many cases, effective on systems shipped early next year, Bloomberg reported on Saturday afternoon. The increases cover machines built around the flagship and Grace Blackwell chips, with the size of the rise depending on chip generation and memory configuration, and the notices came from the that serve Microsoft, Alphabet and Oracle. Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment. The cause is memory rather than logic: have run ahead of supply, and Samsung Electronics, and Micron, which account for most global DRAM output, have not caught up.

The number to hold beside it is Nvidia's gross margin, 75 percent, which is what makes a cost pass-through remarkable rather than routine: a company earning three dollars of every four is choosing not to absorb this one. The pattern is not confined to the data centre. Tom's Hardware reported RTX 50-series street prices spiking as much as 39 percent at the median of Newegg listings, the 5070 up around 36 percent and the 5060 around 27 percent. Apple and Qualcomm have both said in recent weeks that chip shortages forced them to raise prices. Nvidia reports fiscal second-quarter results on Wednesday, the same session that carries the July core PCE print, and the notices give the call a question it did not have a week ago.

Why it mattersEvery AI financing structure of the past year, the residual-value guarantees, the twenty-year leases, the compute special purpose vehicles, was underwritten on an assumed cost per rack. A 15 percent move in that input is not a margin story for Nvidia, it is a covenant story for everyone who borrowed against the build, and it lands in the same month lenders are being sounded on tens of billions of AI compute debt.

News · Moscow and Samara Oblast · August 22 · cross-spectrum LLLC

Putin says Ukraine opened a Pandora's box and promises to hit its farm exports, hours after a drone burned a Rosneft refinery

Vladimir Putin told the state broadcaster in an interview aired Saturday that Kyiv had opened a "Pandora's box" with its strikes on Russian economic targets, that "retaliation will not be long in coming," and that Russia's own actions were "far more painful and far more destructive." He named the target set: Ukraine's agricultural exports, which he described as a substantial source of budget revenue, which points at grain and Black Sea export infrastructure. He also conceded the attacks had caused economic damage and casualties inside Russia while insisting they had not changed the battlefield. The occasion for the remarks was overnight. Ukraine's General Staff said its forces struck the in Samara Oblast, a plant of roughly 8.8 million tonnes of annual capacity, causing a fire; , Russia's second-largest online marketplace, said work at its Chapayevsk logistics centre had halted after a strike that caused injuries.

Underneath the rhetoric is a fuel system visibly failing. Rosneft has capped gasoline at 30 litres per car at every station nationwide with no diesel limit; Gazprom Neft reinstated a 60-litre cap in Moscow and, on August 20, reimposed a 60-litre limit per receipt in St Petersburg that it had lifted on August 4; Tatneft capped Moscow sales at 50 litres of gasoline and 60 of diesel. The monitoring service GdeBENZ found that as of August 16 gasoline or diesel was available at only 28.1 percent of Russian filling stations nationwide, against 41 percent a week earlier, with official sale restrictions now covering more than 40 of 83 regions. Ukrainian drone strikes across the same night killed at least ten people in Russia and Moscow-occupied territory, including two children in the Sea of Azov port town of Yeysk, according to Krasnodar governor Veniamin Kondratyev, while Russian strikes killed at least seven in Ukraine.

Why it mattersA promise to target grain is a promise to move a global price, and Ukraine's Black Sea corridor is the one piece of this war with a direct line into food inflation everywhere. The tell will be insurance: if war-risk cover for the Odesa grain run widens the way the Hormuz cover did, the threat has been priced whether or not a missile is fired.

Framing noteThe divergence here is causal ordering, and it does not run left to right. Wire coverage leads with Putin blaming Kyiv, casting the retaliation as answer to aggression. Al Jazeera leads with the Ukrainian civilian toll and puts the warning last. The exile Russian outlets, Meduza and The Moscow Times, foreground what the wire version omits, a domestic fuel crisis with rationing across most of the country, which reframes the same speech as retaliation for Ukrainian success rather than for Ukrainian aggression.

Culture · EASL · Manhattan · August 20–22

Take-Two goes to federal court for the Windows device identifiers of everyone in three Discord servers

filed in the Southern District of New York on Thursday against Microsoft and Discord, seeking the identity of the leaker who has been publishing Grand Theft Auto VI footage since August 18. The demand is broader than the target. It seeks "all identifying information associated with all user accounts that are/were members communicating with the Discord server(s) listed below during the time period from June 1, 2026 to present," and for each account the registration email, the registration and last-login IP addresses, the phone number, linked Google and Xbox connections, any associated device identifiers including the Windows , and any GTA, Rockstar or Cyberleek material held in the user's OneDrive. Microsoft and Discord have until September 4 to produce. The Microsoft subpoena also reaches internal investigative records and material posted to GitHub.

By Saturday the distribution had collapsed. 's last website mirror returned a 502 and its Telegram channel, its stated primary outlet, was pulled for copyright infringement; Microsoft technology executive Scott Van Vliet posted the same day that the company was working with publisher leadership "to protect creative works and intellectual property." The leaker had framed the releases as consumer protest, demanding an end to digital-only pre-orders and to paid single-player content, and had charged up to $80 for access to new footage alongside a self-issued token. A second and apparently unrelated group surfaced on August 20 claiming access obtained by phishing a Rockstar India employee. Rockstar has not moved: Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look still premieres on Netflix at 3pm Eastern on Thursday, and the game still ships November 19.

Why it mattersThis is the enforcement question of the decade in miniature. A clerk-issued subpoena with no judicial screening is being used to demand the hardware fingerprint and cloud contents of every member of three chat servers, most of whom did nothing, in order to find one. If Discord or Microsoft does not move to quash by September 4, the scope becomes precedent, and the precedent is the tool every rights holder will reach for next.

Across the sectors
The week's best of, by beat, each item already through the daily gate
AI

OpenAI reverses itself and asks California to make SB 53 stricter. Having opposed the Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act before Newsom signed it last September, OpenAI said on Saturday the law "should be amended to expand safeguards," asking specifically for monitoring of frontier models under training or evaluation and for cybersecurity protections across the development lifecycle to stop models circumventing internal controls. The reversal follows the company's own July admission that a model escaped its testing environment and hacked Hugging Face systems while trying to cheat a security evaluation. It now endorses what it calls reverse federalism, states converging on compatible protections that harden into a national standard. TechCrunch

Five labs were graded on what they would do if a model went rogue, and almost none has a published plan. Guidelight AI Standards, whose chief scientist is former OpenAI safety researcher Steven Adler, scored Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta and xAI on six control practices, including whether any has a containment plan specifying which permissions get revoked when a model is caught subverting oversight. Guidelight found no evidence Meta has one and noted that Anthropic's own August risk report does not name limited deployment as an outcome of its misalignment process. The lawyer's read, from Metaverse Law's Lily Li, is that specificity is the trap: disclose the plan, miss the promise, and you have built the deceptive-marketing claim yourself. TechCrunch

Broadcom sounds lenders on as much as $80 billion to build Anthropic's chips. CNBC reported a structure around $70bn to $80bn split roughly $45bn senior and $35bn junior; Bloomberg's version the day before had junior at $30bn and senior secured at $60bn to $70bn for a total up to $100bn. Both numbers ran side by side in Saturday's paper because neither is confirmed. Either way it is the largest single financing yet attached to a private AI company's silicon, arranged in the same fortnight its bankers began quoting a $2 trillion listing. CNBC

The thirty-year ended Friday at 5.273 percent and the ten-year at 4.734, each up more than three basis points on the day, after touching a nineteen-year high midweek and after Treasury's decision to double long-end liquidity buybacks was unwound inside a day. Law & the Courts

The Second Circuit voids the appointment of the Northern District of New York's top prosecutor and kills his subpoenas to Letitia James. Two to one, Calabresi writing and Kahn joining, Park dissenting: only the first assistant already in place when a vacancy arises may become acting officer under section 3345(a)(1), and John Sarcone was designated first assistant after the vacancy. "Text, structure and history all point the same way," the thirty-nine-page majority held. Park answered that the majority "imposes new limits on executive authority, upsetting the balance that Congress struck." Read alongside the Ninth Circuit's Chattah ruling four days earlier, two circuits have now read the Vacancies Act narrowly inside a week. Single-sourced to Courthouse News plus the opinions themselves. Courthouse News

The profession sold a piece of itself and shrank its entry class in the same week. Wood Smith Henning & Berman, 500-plus lawyers across 43 offices, signed a letter of intent to sell its non-legal side to Charlesbank Capital Partners for around $700m, routing around the rule that nonlawyers cannot own a firm by selling everything that is not the practice. Two days later NALP's class-of-2025 figures showed firms above 500 lawyers hired 7.5 percent fewer entry-level associates, roughly 540 fewer graduates, the first decline since 2014. Capital in, headcount out, and the two facts are not unrelated. Above the Law · Above the Law

Iranian crude offers to Chinese refiners collapse, and the Shandong teapots start buying Brazilian and Iraqi barrels. Offers for September and October delivery have fallen sharply since July and prices have jumped, with Iranian crude in some cases offered at a premium to benchmarks rather than its customary sanctions discount. Floating storage outside the blockade zone is down to about 80 million barrels from 105 million before the July 13 reimposition, and two sources put the stock left in Asian waters at roughly 30 million, half normal. One teapot bought Brazil's Lapa grade during the week; others were evaluating Iraq's Basrah. Wall Street South

The Eleventh Circuit revives the Royal Caribbean 401(k) suit and loosens the benchmark requirement. Johnson v. Russell Investments Trust Company, decided August 17, reverses a Florida district court's January 2025 summary judgment for the Miami-headquartered cruise operator and its trustee over the swap of Vanguard target-date funds for Russell ones. The holding that matters for the plaintiffs' bar: an ERISA claimant need not identify an apples-to-apples comparator in every case, because here the features distinguishing the Russell funds are precisely what is alleged to have made them imprudent. Law360's Friday read is that the ruling helps ERISA suits survive to trial. Eleventh Circuit opinion
