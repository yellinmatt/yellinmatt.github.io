The Splash · Corporate / AI · Santa Clara and New York · September 3

Nvidia is buying the place models are downloaded from, and for the first time in a year it has signed a deal a merger regulator gets to read

Nvidia agreed to buy for $12,930,300,000, the company said Thursday, a figure it published to the dollar in a post signed by Jensen Huang and confirmed in an 8-K covering an agreement dated September 2. The structure separates roughly $11.9 billion payable to Hugging Face stockholders from an equity of up to about $1 billion for employees joining Nvidia, and the parties expect to close in the first half of 2027. It is the second-largest purchase in Nvidia's history, behind the $20 billion it paid for Groq's assets last December, and nearly double the $6.9 billion deal, the largest acquisition it has ever actually completed.

What Nvidia is buying is a chokepoint rather than a technology. Hugging Face is where open models are published and downloaded, and Huang's post counts more than 18 million developers, over 3 million models, 500,000 datasets, a million applications and 200,000 companies on the platform. Nvidia says it is already the largest contributor of open models there, with more than 500 of its own and 250 open datasets. The announcement then makes a set of commitments written for a reviewer rather than a reader: the platform "will remain an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem," Nvidia compute "will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging Face," and multi-cloud and multi-accelerator development will continue to be supported. Clement Delangue, who has spent years positioning the company as neutral ground between the labs, told CNBC that he approached Huang rather than the reverse.

NVIDIA compute will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging Face.
— Jensen Huang, Nvidia, September 3

Those covenants exist because this deal, unlike the last two, has to be filed. The Groq transaction was an asset and licensing purchase that left the legal shell alive and produced no change of control; buying Hugging Face outright triggers notification in Washington and merger review in Brussels, and hands both a theory with a decided precedent behind it. Nvidia's own $40 billion bid for Arm collapsed in 2022 under that argument, that a supplier everyone depends on should not own the layer everyone depends on. Justin Boitano, Nvidia's vice president and general manager for enterprise computing, has begun pre-arguing the case in public, calling the combination a deconcentration platform and predicting regulators "are going to see this as overwhelmingly positive." The question a reviewer will actually test is narrower: whether AMD's stack stays a first-class backend on the default download path.

Why it mattersNvidia spent the past year acquiring capability through structures built to avoid a filing, and it has now bought something it cannot. The commons where open models live is about to belong to the company that sells the accelerators they run on, and the fight will be over backend parity and download telemetry rather than price.

The World · Kharg Island and the Gulf of Oman · September 5 · cross-spectrum: L+LL+C+LR+R

Three Iranian tankers for two American warships: CENTCOM puts a price on being shot at

United States forces disabled two Iranian crude carriers and sank a third on Saturday, Central Command said, after the launched ballistic missiles toward two Navy warships patrolling regional waters. An aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer evaded multiple attacks and no American personnel were harmed, according to CENTCOM. The M/T Downy was permanently disabled off , the M/T Stark 1 near Jask on the Gulf of Oman side of the strait, and the M/T Kylo, sailing empty, was struck in several places and sunk after CENTCOM says its crew was directed to abandon ship. Air Force and Navy fighters and drones flew the package.

Adm. Brad Cooper, the CENTCOM commander, described the arithmetic himself. "Let the message to the IRGC be clear: If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost, taking out three of yours," he said, adding that the United States would "destroy Iran's limited and exposed oil fleet" if the attacks continue. The three vessels were described as part of a multibillion-dollar funding the Guard and its regional proxies. By Friday, Central Command counted more than seventy tankers held from entering or leaving Iranian ports, over 166 million barrels and more than $13 billion of cargo, with seventy-five blockade-runners turned around since the campaign resumed. Iran's foreign ministry called the strikes illegal, aggressive and a war crime; the IRGC said it had targeted three tankers transiting Hormuz on "unauthorized" routes along with three American-affiliated ships and a naval drone, and warned other masters that "you will be targeted."

Why it mattersThe administration has settled on a retaliation currency that is neither diplomatic nor conventionally military: each Iranian attack is answered on Tehran's balance sheet rather than at its bases. It keeps the exchange inside the commercial domain, and it also means the escalation ladder is now denominated in barrels that the rest of the world buys.

Framing noteFox News reports the strike as initiative, headlining that US forces "target 3 Iranian oil tankers in response to IRGC missile attack" and leading on Cooper's cost quote; the Associated Press copy carried by The Hill and HuffPost declines to assert the Iranian launch as fact, headlining the same events as strikes "after saying warships were targeted." The Washington Post splits it, attributing throughout.

Markets & Deals · Washington and New York · September 4

A 162,000 payroll print against a 53,000 forecast puts the two-year at its highest since January 2025

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 162,000 nonfarm payrolls added in August against a consensus near 53,000, with the unemployment rate steady at 4.1 percent, and the front end of the Treasury curve repriced within the hour. The two-year yield reached 4.377 percent intraday and extended to 4.425 percent, the highest since January 2025; the ten-year rose about three basis points to 4.79 percent, reversing two sessions of declines; the thirty-year barely moved at 5.245 percent. That is a , not a duration selloff: with the funds target at 3.50 to 3.75 percent, a 4.425 percent two-year is pricing well more than one move. CME futures put the odds of a 25 basis point hike at the September 15 and 16 meeting at 58 percent, roughly nine points higher than the day before.

The reversal inside a single week is the part worth holding onto. On Wednesday morning Governor said he would be inclined to hold if disinflation persisted, citing a rate that had slipped from 4.76 percent in February to 3.05 percent; hike odds fell from 62.4 percent to 50.4 percent on the remark. Friday erased it. Equities took the print poorly, with the Dow closing at 53,414.25, down 0.51 percent, the S&P 500 at 7,718.60 and the Nasdaq at 26,506.99, while the VIX stayed calm at 14.53. No coupon auction settled in the window, the week after a refunding cycle being reliably empty; the last was the $44 billion seven-year at 4.512 percent on August 27, and the next three-year, ten-year and thirty-year cycle begins this week.

Why it mattersEvery convertible priced this quarter and every leveraged buyout modelled this year discounts off a front end that has now moved seven basis points on one employment report. inherits a market that is now pricing him to tighten into a 4.1 percent unemployment rate.

AI · San Francisco and London · September 4

A machine wrote 13 million lines of proof in eleven days, and Fermat's Last Theorem is now checked

Anthropic published on Friday what it says is the first end-to-end, computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, produced by Claude working largely autonomously over eleven days. The output runs to roughly 13 million lines of , more than five times the size of , and contains 30,300 proved theorems of which 29,500 are load-bearing in the final result. The model was an internal system Anthropic describes as roughly comparable to Claude Fable 5.1, run as dozens of parallel agents against a coordination tool called Prove2Me that maintains a directed graph of theorem statements so agents can pick the next available target. Earlier attempts had failed because the agents lost track of the project's state and stopped collaborating; those runs still contributed about seven percent of the non-boilerplate lines.

Kevin Buzzard, who has led the human formalization effort since 2024 and is funded to work on it through 2029, reviewed the result and confirmed it "proves Fermat's Last Theorem with no assumptions other than the axioms of mathematics." The check used Lean's three standard axioms and a comparator tool confirmed the statement matches Mathlib's own. Scale sets the achievement: Andrew Wiles's 1995 proof runs 129 pages, the community blueprint for the initial phase of formalizing it alone runs 86, and the work was expected to take years. Buzzard's second observation is the one to keep. "Such techniques will lead to new tools, rooting out errors in the current mathematical corpus and lightening the load of referees," he wrote, in a post titled "FLT: Anthropic has beaten me to it." Three researchers on personal Claude subscriptions used the same platform to formalize Vinogradov's Three Primes Theorem in three days.

Why it mattersMathematics is the first verification profession to have its checking work automated, and its response was relief rather than alarm, because the ground truth is mechanical and the referee shortage was real. Law has neither property: there is no compiler for a brief, so the profession will get the labour displacement without the certainty that made mathematicians welcome it.

Entertainment & EASL · Baton Rouge and Birmingham · September 3

The Southeastern Conference sued one of its own members, and claimed a First Amendment right not to be associated with it

Judge William Jorden of the East Baton Rouge Parish court granted a preliminary injunction on Thursday barring the NCAA and the Southeastern Conference from punishing any school that signs and plays the 42 athletes suing in Louisiana for a fifth season. Within hours the conference sued Louisiana State University in Alabama federal court, naming the Board of Supervisors, president Wade Rousse, athletic director Verge Ausberry and head coach Lane Kiffin, and alleging that LSU "waged a deliberate and coordinated campaign to recruit professional athletes." The theory is , the right not to associate: the conference asks for an injunction "prohibiting the defendants from violating the conference's First Amendment rights by compelling the conference to associate with conduct that is contrary to the Conference's purpose and mission."

The rule at issue was one week old. testified in Baton Rouge that bars on returning professionals date to at least 1911, and his affidavit records the conference presidents voting 15 to nothing to adopt it, with LSU's Rousse abstaining. The players in question are Dae'Quan Wright and Zxavian Harris, both former Ole Miss athletes who played for Kiffin there and both of whom have signed NFL contracts. Facing a Friday , LSU submitted 103 players, left both men off, and added a Notre Dame transfer instead; Kiffin cited "the uncertainties of what could come." Because both are enrolled, either can still be added later this season. LSU opened against Clemson on Saturday.

Why it mattersCollege sport has spent five years losing antitrust cases, and the conference has now stopped defending its rules as procompetitive and started defending them as speech. If a private association can characterize its eligibility rules as expression, the doctrine that has dismantled amateurism since Alston becomes much harder to apply.

Law & the Courts · Boston and Washington · September 4

A district judge entered a new injunction on Friday night, and the Solicitor General's Supreme Court application was moot by 7 p.m.

Judge Indira Talwani of the District of Massachusetts granted a preliminary injunction on Friday barring the Postal Service from requiring states to comply with the ballot-envelope-design and voter-data-portal provisions of its new Final Rule, replacing the temporary restraining order she entered on August 27. Implementation less than seventy days before the November election "threatens disenfranchisement of millions of United States citizens who seek to vote by mail," she wrote, and the record "includes no evidence relating to fraudulent mail voting to support the rushed implementation of the Final Rule." The underlying suit is a challenge by 23 states led by California, plus the District of Columbia, to the March executive order on citizenship verification in federal elections; the order bars implementation in those 24 jurisdictions on or before November 3.

The Solicitor General had gone to the Supreme Court the day before. filed in United States Postal Service v. California shortly before four o'clock Thursday, seeking a stay and an on the ground that the restraining order "partially nullifies the Postal Service's efforts to address the risk that the federal mails will be used to perpetrate voter fraud," and warning that North Carolina and Alabama would begin mailing ballots as soon as September 4: "Once those ballot envelopes enter the mailstream, there is no retrieving them." The Court set a response deadline of ten o'clock Tuesday morning. Friday night, California's deputy solicitor general wrote the Clerk to say the application was , the temporary order it attacked having been dissolved into a preliminary injunction. The administration says it will appeal to the First Circuit.

Why it mattersThis is procedure used as substance, and used well. A district judge who converts a restraining order into a preliminary injunction while an emergency application against it is pending does not win the merits; she wins the two weeks the government needed, inside a window that shortens every day.
