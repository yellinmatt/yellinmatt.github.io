The Nasdaq falls 5.52% into a correction as investors reprice the AI buildout after the Democratic sweep

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52 percent on Thursday, its worst day since April 2025, and closed in , at least 10 percent below its Oct. 6 record. The S&P 500 lost 4.36 percent and the Dow gave up 1,833.06 points, or 3.44 percent, to 51,523.06. Reuters said chip stocks posted their second-biggest fall on record, with the down 12 percent, and that some $2 trillion came off the Nasdaq’s market value.

“This feels like the capitulation phase for the data center capex beneficiary stocks, especially the semiconductors.”
Jed Ellerbroek, Argent Capital Management, to Reuters via Fox Business, Nov. 5

The trigger was the Democratic sweep of Congress, read a day late. Markets rose Wednesday as the unwound; by Thursday investors were pricing a buildout that would be “slower and more expensive,” in the words of eToro’s Jakub Rochlitz. The Bank of England hikes to 4% as the selloff bull-steepens Treasuries before payrolls

The Bank of England raised its rate to 4 percent from 3.75 percent on Thursday, on an 8-1 vote with the dissenter seeking a half point, Financial News reported, its first increase since 2023, Reuters said. The bank projects inflation peaking at 4.9 percent in the second quarter of 2027. “Monetary policy cannot influence global energy prices,” Governor Andrew Bailey said.

In New York the selloff bought Treasuries. The Treasury’s par curve put the two-year at 4.46 percent from 4.58, the 10-year at 4.84 from 4.90 and the 30-year at 5.25 from 5.28, a sharp , Reuters said, with the next Fed hike pushed to .
