Democrats win the House and the Senate in a rebuke of Trump, and the subpoena power changes hands

Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in Tuesday’s midterm elections, taking the Senate late in the evening and the House around 1:30 a.m. Wednesday, in what the Associated Press called “a decisive rebuke of Republican governance in Washington.” Republicans entered the night holding the Senate 53-47. The Boston Globe’s 2:58 a.m. count put Democrats at 54 Senate seats and ahead 224 to 134 in the House, with about 77 House seats unallocated.

“I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!”

Gina Hinojosa beat Gov. Greg Abbott of Texas, and Amy Acton beat Vivek Ramaswamy in Ohio. Florida held for Republicans, with Sen. Ashley Moody and Rep. Byron Donalds, the governor-elect. Alaska, Mississippi, Montana and Nebraska were uncalled in the Globe’s count.

AP reported that Democrats plan investigations of senior officials and of how Trump has enriched his family, and that impeachment “is not off the table.” promised a 100-day agenda on the cost of living and oversight. Trump, who vowed to veto Democratic bills, told Fox News voters “didn’t like the candidates” and said he would accept the results. Sen.
