Whether a 401(k) can be sued over its alternatives
Law · Washington

The justices take up Intel’s hedge fund and private equity bets in its 401(k)

The Supreme Court hears Anderson v. Intel Corp. Investment Policy Committee, No. 25-498, on Tuesday morning. The question is whether an ERISA plaintiff alleging from underperformance must plead a Intel’s plans allocated heavily to hedge funds and private equity, and employees say that meant higher fees and lower returns than peers. The Ninth Circuit held that “allegations of inferior returns or higher costs alone are insufficient.”

Matthew Wessler of argues for the employees, and the Solicitor General shares Intel’s time. The Investment Company Institute and two plan-sponsor groups filed for Intel, whose position is that the duty concerns “process, not performance.” The same committee was before the Court in 2020, in Sulyma. A decision is due by early July.

Why it mattersThe SEC opened retail access to private markets last week. If alternatives have no benchmark, a rule requiring one decides whether putting them in a 401(k) can ever be tested in court.

Meloni filed a four-second recording of her own voice as an EU trade mark

Giorgia Meloni has applied to register her voice as an EU trade mark, Reuters reported on Monday. The specimen at the is a four-second recording of her saying “Io sono Giorgia Meloni” twice, the line that gave her 2021 memoir its title. The application is under examination. Italian media noted that registration would not by itself stop AI clones but could add legal obstacles.
