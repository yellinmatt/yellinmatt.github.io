A contractor’s router, not a cyberattack, most likely grounded every U.S. flight, Duffy says, and he wants $50 billion to replace the network

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on Sunday that a router installed in a contractor’s upgrade most likely caused Friday’s nationwide , and he told CBS’s Face the Nation that the Federal Aviation Administration has to “get off old network, old telecom.” The FAA halted every U.S. departure at 3:36 p.m. Eastern on Nov. 13 with about 3,500 flights in the air. FlightAware counted about 9,159 cancellations and 7,190 delays that day, CBS reported.

But what I don’t have money for is a new telecom system.
Sean Duffy, Face the Nation, Nov. 15

Crews from were replacing at a remote FAA site when a router began sending about 1,000 log-on attempts every four to five seconds, the AP reported. “We think it was the installation or the router that was the bad actor,” Duffy said on Fox News Sunday, adding that there was no indication of a cyberattack. The FBI remains involved. On NBC’s Meet the Press he said he wants to move away from L3Harris, “but they own the network.”

Duffy said more than 70 percent of copper lines and almost 470 radios have been switched over, and he has asked Congress for $50 billion. Congress allocated for FAA upgrades in 2025, of which about $1 billion has been spent, the AP reported, and a September GAO report found the agency had missed at least four of 11 modernization recommendations. The meets on Monday on preventing a repeat.

Why it mattersThe worst air-traffic outage in decades traces to a routine cutover on a network the government does not own, and the fight over who pays to replace it opens with a $50 billion ask.

Glass Lewis’s chief argues that dual-class control should carry an expiry date

Bob Mann, chief executive of , argued on Sunday that “one-share-one-vote should remain the default,” in a post on the Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance. He wrote that investors price voting limits into the bundle they buy at an IPO and that permanence is the core problem with multi-class structures. “An exceptional founder may justify exceptional treatment,” he wrote.

His remedy is the , triggered by time, the founder’s departure, the founder’s economic stake falling below a threshold, or a succession event. He cited Britain’s and noted that exchanges compete for listings, naming Meta and Alphabet as examples. He offered no data and did not address any pending offering. “The durability of control should bear some relationship to the durability of the circumstances,” he wrote.

Why it mattersEvery dual-class charter now coming to market, including the Class F structure in this week’s Anthropic offering, is drafted against this argument, and it tells deal lawyers which terms proxy advisers will punish.

The justices release orders at 9:30 after Friday’s conference, with Alabama’s congressional map and the 9/11 families’ Taliban cases among the relists

The Supreme Court is scheduled to release orders at 9:30 a.m. on Monday after its Nov. 13 conference. Israel’s attorney general and police are reported to be examining whether Netanyahu’s office staged election-day vote-buying arrests

Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara and the police Investigations and Intelligence Division are examining whether the Prime Minister’s Office used the police to undermine the Oct. 27 election, Channel 12’s reported on Sunday, according to the Times of Israel. The prime minister’s office said “criminal information” reached Norden and called other claims defamatory. Gadi Eisenkot said Netanyahu must not remain “for even one minute longer” and pledged a commission of inquiry; Likud called it “outrageous incitement.” Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats, and neither bloc holds . Why it mattersAn inquiry into whether a sitting government turned the police on an election would hang over coalition talks that already lack a majority, with the attorney general the cabinet tried to dismiss at its center.
