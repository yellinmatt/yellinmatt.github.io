Quebecor makes an unsolicited bid worth about C$31 billion for Telus, which says it has “serious concerns”

Quebecor has made an unsolicited cash-and-stock proposal for Telus worth about C$31 billion, and Telus said on Monday that it has “serious concerns.” The offer is C$6.35 in cash plus 0.17 Quebecor Class B shares for each Telus share. A Nov. 9 revision raised the from 0.16 and dropped a planned sale of Telus Health.

Telus said the price undervalues it, that Quebecor has not said what regulatory remedies it would accept and that leverage of about 4.1 times 2027 EBITDA is too high, and that Telus holders would supply most of the value while Quebecor’s ran the board. A chaired by John Manley proposed a meeting on Nov. 23.

Why it mattersThe largest bid of the window is a hostile one in a three-carrier regulated market, and the fight will turn on governance and remedies as much as on price.

Nvidia’s board is not weighing a Huang return, reports say, hours before its first earnings without him

Nvidia’s board is not considering reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive, Politico, Reuters and CNBC reported on Monday, a day before the company reports fiscal third-quarter results under interim chief executive at about 4:20 p.m. Eastern. President Trump said Huang “learned a big lesson” and that he would not mind a return.

A petition to bring Huang back passed 186,000 signatures, including more than 8,800 current employees, CBS and Forbes reported. Nvidia guided to revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2 percent, assuming no China data-center sales, against an LSEG consensus near $109.2 billion. Three House committee chairs have asked for a closed-door on Wednesday.

Why it mattersNvidia’s first earnings without its founder test whether a board can hold a succession line against the president, a petition and a fifth of its own staff.
