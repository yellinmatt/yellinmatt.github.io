Iran offers to reopen Hormuz within two days on seven conditions, as its new supreme leader orders retaliation without limits

Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said on Sunday that Qatari mediators have proposed reopening the within two days if Washington meets Tehran’s terms, with the United States acting on the first day and shipping resuming on the second, The Tribune reported, citing Al Jazeera. An earlier draft allowed five days. No direct talks between the two governments are planned.

The terms run to seven: lifting the sanctions reimposed after the Islamabad memorandum collapsed in July, a smaller American military presence, restored oil waivers, released frozen funds, recognition of a new route built with Oman, the closure of , and an end to the war on all fronts, Lebanon included. President Trump said Iran wants a deal and that Washington is in no hurry, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said no decision on new strikes has been made.

Tehran paired the offer with a threat. On Saturday , who succeeded his assassinated father, ordered an “immediate, impactful, and geographically unrestricted response” to any attack, Fox News reported, and a member of parliament’s security committee said ten bills to quit the have been filed. says its blockade has turned back 144 commercial ships, and December Brent settled at $117.64 on Friday after the Revolutionary Guard seized four tankers.

Reopening the Strait of Hormuz in accordance with our conditions paves the way for the resumption of negotiations.
Abbas Araghchi, via The Tribune, November 1

Why it mattersOil near $118 prices a closed strait, so the offer is a trade with two legs: a fast reopening would unwind much of the premium, and a strike after Tuesday’s midterms would add to it. The sixth condition is the tell.

Law / EASL · Miami · South Florida · October 31 · single-tier coverage: C

A Miami federal judge throws out Trump’s suit against the Wall Street Journal over the Epstein letter, this time with prejudice

U.S. District Judge Darrin P. Gayles dismissed President Trump’s defamation suit against Dow Jones, News Corp, Rupert Murdoch and the reporters on Saturday, ruling that the amended complaint again failed to , the Associated Press reported. The 20-page order says the suit was “grasping at straws” and tried to “manufacture” flaws in the Journal’s reporting on a birthday letter to Jeffrey Epstein bearing Trump’s name.

Gayles threw out the first complaint in April, finding it came “nowhere close” to the standard, and let Trump replead. A spokesperson said the president “will continue to press forward,” and CBS News cited a source saying an appeal is likely. Coverage disagrees on the damages sought, so this paper prints no figure until the amended complaint is read.

Why it mattersDismissal at the pleading stage is the media bar’s best result in a public-figure case, and an appeal would test Sullivan in the circuit that hears Florida’s press cases.

AI / Markets · San Francisco · October 31 · conflict disclosed

OpenAI cuts its funding target by a third to $20 billion after a tepid response from some large investors

OpenAI has lowered the target for its new round to about $20 billion from $30 billion, at a $1.25 trillion valuation, the Financial Times reported on Friday, and the is likely to land below that, TechCrunch reported on Saturday. The company first pitched investors at $1.4 trillion. The Wall Street Journal reported that only $11 billion is secured and that some large investors were “tepid.”

Investors from the United Arab Emirates, including the state-backed fund MGX, lead the round, and OpenAI would receive an in the region. Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia and Andreessen Horowitz also plan to invest, and a later offering for wealthy individuals would come at the same price. OpenAI is aiming for a 2027 listing and wants the round closed before Anthropic goes public, Reuters reported. Conflict disclosed: this paper is compiled with Anthropic’s model.

Why it mattersThe markdown came the week Anthropic filed to list and the Nasdaq fell 2.9% in a day, so the private price of the leading lab is now being set by public comparables and Gulf capacity.

News / World · Paris · October 31 · cross-spectrum CLR

France’s third weekend of youth-led protests draws 320,000, and a firebomb sets a Paris riot officer alight

About 320,000 people marched across France on Saturday, 70,000 of them in Paris, by the authorities’ count, against organisers’ claim of nearly 500,000, Agence France-Presse reported. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez reported 520 arrests by 9 p.m. and called the firebombing of a riot officer an “attempted murder.” Official turnout has fallen from about 550,000 on Oct. 17 and 410,000 on Oct. 24.

The movement began with high-school students protesting crumbling schools, teacher shortages and , and has since drawn the and the unions. A curfew from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. covers République and Châtelet-Les Halles through Sunday. Marine Le Pen, who leads presidential polling, demanded the attackers be “punished with the utmost severity.”

Why it mattersFitch has moved France’s outlook to negative and the economy shrank in the third quarter, so a government six months from a presidential vote cannot spend its way to calm, and the bond spread carries the risk.

News / World · Jerusalem · November 1 · single-tier coverage: C

Israel’s Arab parties are set to back Eisenkot, leaving him one seat short of a majority before Herzog’s talks

The Joint List and , with 19 seats between them, are expected to recommend Gadi Eisenkot for prime minister, a senior Joint List official told Walla on Sunday, the Jerusalem Post reported. Ynet’s tally puts Eisenkot at 60 recommendations to Benjamin Netanyahu’s 46, with Yisrael Beiteinu and United Torah Judaism, seven seats each, undecided. A majority is 61.

Avigdor Liberman’s seven would carry Eisenkot to 67, but Ynet warns that a government resting on both Arab parties and Liberman would be fragile, and Netanyahu has kept a unity option open. President Isaac Herzog receives the official results on Wednesday and must by Nov. 11.

Why it mattersWhoever forms Israel’s next government inherits the Iran war at its most delicate point, and an Eisenkot coalition would depend on Arab parties for the first time since 2021.

Entertainment · Los Angeles · October 31

A24’s You Can See Everything edges Clayface for No. 1 on a quiet Halloween weekend

A24’s You Can See Everything was on course for about $10.6 million this weekend, its first time at No. 1, against $10.2 million for Warner Bros.’ Clayface, according to Saturday estimates reported by TheWrap. It has about $63 million domestic, second among October releases to Amazon MGM’s Verity. Clayface, down 57%, has $44 million after ten days on a reported $40 million budget. Black Bear’s Wicker, for $2.5 million, has grossed $13.2 million. No wide release opened, and Sunday figures follow.

Why it mattersWicker’s fivefold return on a festival price is the number buyers will cite at the next market, and Clayface’s slide puts a studio franchise bet under review.
