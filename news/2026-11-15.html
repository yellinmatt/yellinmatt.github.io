The week’s single biggest story
The Splash · AI / Markets · Santa Clara · November 14 · single-tier coverage: C

A petition backed by Elon Musk asks Nvidia’s board to bring back Jensen Huang, two days after he resigned

A petition calling on Nvidia’s board to reinstate Jensen Huang had more than 115,000 signatures by Saturday afternoon, TechCrunch reported, two days after the co-founder, who had run the company since 1993, resigned on Thursday as chief executive and took over on an interim basis. Elon Musk, Replit’s Amjad Masad and Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney were among the named signers.

The site says signers are “concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process” and asks the to bring him back while an establishes the facts. Its own breakdown counts 5,965 current employees, 1,108 former employees and 63,063 shareholders, about 70,000 of the total, and every signature is self-reported. The inquiry followed reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort through a relationship with a chip executive whom U.S. intelligence identified as a suspected Chinese spy. The shares fell 2% on Friday.

Governments that depend on the chips moved to steady supply. South Korea’s science ministry said Nvidia had reaffirmed its plan to deliver after an emergency meeting of its AI strategy council, and Taiwan’s economy minister, Kung Ming-shin, said plans for an overseas headquarters in Taipei remain on track, TechCrunch reported. Singapore said its inquiry into , a local server reseller, and six people continues with limited help from Washington. Nvidia is due to report quarterly results this week.

Jensen deserves due process, not mob justice.
The Stand with Jensen petition, via TechCrunch, November 14

Why it mattersA petition binds no director, but its signers include partners and holders the board answers to, and it lands days before earnings. Removing a founder without a high-vote class was the easy part; keeping him out is now the board’s harder call.

SpaceX and Tesla merger talks are progressing, Bloomberg reports, and the S&P 500 is part of the prize

Talks to combine SpaceX and Tesla have been progressing, Bloomberg News reported on Saturday, and a merger would give SpaceX a way into the , which denied it a quick place months ago, because Tesla is one of the index’s largest members. Bloomberg cited synergies and the cost of robots, orbital data centers and Mars. No terms have been reported.

Musk said on Oct. 20 that both companies had formed , that he is recused, and that Tesla would need “50% of all shareholders, rather than simply a majority of those voting.” SpaceX was then worth about $2 trillion and Tesla nearly $1.5 trillion. Bloomberg reported in October that the aim is a deal in the first half of 2027 that closes while President Trump is in office. Musk controls more than 80% of SpaceX’s votes and owns about 20% of Tesla.

Why it mattersAt about $3.5 trillion it would be the largest combination on record: a controller on both sides, a recused chief executive and an index rule as a motive.

The CFTC sues Arkansas to stop its case against Kalshi as a Louisiana judge orders prediction markets out of the state

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission asked a federal court in Arkansas late Friday to block the state’s enforcement action against Kalshi, arguing that regulating prediction markets is “exclusively” a federal job, Law360 reported. A Louisiana state judge has separately ordered Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase and several other platforms to “temporarily cease operations in the state” in the attorney general’s suit over sports-event contracts sold without a gambling license.

The commission has now sued about ten states, Utah among them. The 2 to 1 against Kalshi, with the Third Circuit for it and the Sixth and Ninth against, and New Jersey’s petition, No. 26-299, is pending, with Kalshi’s response due Dec. 7, Steve Vladeck wrote. Kalshi is a .

News · Washington · November 13 · single-tier coverage: C

Rubio tells the Federalist Society that Americans are “struggling” under the Iran war and attacks the “weaponization of international law”

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, giving the keynote at the Federalist Society’s on Friday evening, defended the war with Iran as necessary to stop an Iranian bomb while conceding it had pushed up gasoline and other costs: “I think we have to recognize that people are struggling. There’s no doubt about it,” the AP reported.

He attacked the United Nations, the and other “global elite” bodies, and defended the strikes on alleged drug boats, the January capture of Nicolás Maduro and the immigration crackdown. The Times of Israel reported him saying further strikes on Iran were unnecessary because of the economic pain already inflicted.

Why it mattersRubio pitched the war and the break with international courts as a legal program to the bar that staffs it, and he conceded the cost at home after Democrats took Congress.

Washington blames a contractor’s cable upgrade, not the new AI tool, for Friday’s nationwide ground stop

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on Saturday that “routine maintenance” grounded U.S. flights on Friday: was replacing copper wire with fiber at a remote site at 3 p.m., and a router then overloaded a critical communications system, TechCrunch reported. The FAA’s acting technology chief, Rebecca Guy, said two sources were pinging the network “a thousand connections over every four to five seconds” until both were disabled.

Administrator Bryan Bedford said there was “zero evidence of anything nefarious and zero evidence of anything AI-related.” Cirium counted more than 8,200 cancellations on Friday evening and at least 3,000 on Saturday, in the this century. Duffy said the timing of such work will be reevaluated and pressed Congress for equipment money.
