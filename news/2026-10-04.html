News · São Paulo · October 4 · cross-spectrum LLLCR

Brazil votes with Lula’s fourth term and the pro-Trump bloc’s reach across Latin America on the same ballot

Polls opened Sunday morning with Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, seeking a fourth non-consecutive term against , 45, a senator for Rio de Janeiro and the son of a former president now serving 27 years for attempting a coup. A follows on 25 October if neither clears half the vote. The same ballot elects 27 governors, all 513 federal deputies and 54 senators.

Washington is on the ballot indirectly. In July the administration put 25% tariffs on roughly $7 billion of Brazilian exports and barred most new permanent visa requests. Brazilian farm exports to China topped $55 billion in 2025, and Flávio Bolsonaro has proposed a United States free-trade agreement and pledged to join the American-led anti-cartel coalition operating across the region. The State Department suspended consular services on the eve of the vote citing security concerns, and police later said they found no evidence corroborating the reported risks.

Why it mattersLatin America’s last large left government is being asked whether a government in the region can still price Washington’s displeasure, and a runoff puts three more weeks of it into the market.

Framing noteThe Guardian leads on the father’s “27-year sentence for attempting a coup”; Fox News leads on Lula as “the 80-year-old, who served time in jail for corruption in a massive payback bribery scandal.” Same election, two different criminal records chosen as the frame.

Corporate · Washington · October 2

Payrolls came in at 29,000 and the thirty-year yield rose anyway

September non-farm payrolls came in at 29,000 against a consensus above 80,000, and unemployment rose to 4.2% from 4.1%. Treasury’s official par curve then closed higher across its whole length: the two-year at 4.83%, up five basis points, the ten-year at 5.28%, up four, the thirty-year at 5.63%, up two. The ten-year had touched 5.34% earlier in the week, its highest since 2002. Odds of a hike on 28 October collapsed to 17% on CME FedWatch from about 36% a week earlier, and to 18% on Kalshi from nearly 70%. December is still priced above 75%.

Note where the selling was. Yields rose most at the front end and least at the long bond, which is rather than a growth scare. Dallas Fed president said higher market-driven rates are helping the Fed tighten. There was no coupon auction in the window; the last thirty-year cleared at 5.308% on 10 September, so the long bond now trades 32 basis points through its own most recent stop. UK thirty-year gilts hit 6.02%, the highest since 1998.

Why it mattersEvery deal priced this quarter, Paramount’s above all, discounts off a curve that keeps rising on bad news. That is a market pricing deficits and AI capital demand, not the policy path.

AI · Washington · October 3–4

Trump makes his spy chief the AI czar and gives a new Super Intelligence Force 120 days

Trump named Director of National Intelligence Walter “Jay” Clayton to a newly created role leading a body called the Super Intelligence Force, keeping the intelligence job alongside it. He chaired the Securities and Exchange Commission from 2017 to 2020, and is joined by FTC chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon under secretary for research and engineering Emil Michael, and of the Office of Personnel Management, all reporting to Trump and chief of staff Susie Wiles. The charter is an assessment of the technology’s risks within 120 days and a recommendation on what role the federal government should play.

Three of the four come out of securities enforcement, antitrust or venture capital rather than national security or AI research. A body staffed like a financial-regulatory task force is a tell about the enforcement theory Washington expects to land on: securities, consumer protection and disclosure law rather than a bespoke AI statute. It follows the voluntary accord six labs signed at the White House on 29 September, built on internal controls, an external auditor and a board committee.

Why it mattersIf frontier AI is policed through disclosure and consumer-protection law rather than a new statute, the lawyers who matter are securities and FTC lawyers, which is a different career map.

Law · Washington · October 4

The Supreme Court opens its term tomorrow with half a docket and a heavier emergency calendar

October Term 2026 opens Monday. SCOTUSblog’s own counter shows 30 cases granted, which the Christian Science Monitor puts at about half the Court’s usual caseload for a term. Monday brings Suncor Energy v. County Commissioners of Boulder County at 10 a.m. and Johnson v. United States Congress at 11, and an order list at 9:30 carrying the ’s dispositions. Fox News reports the Justice Department has won more than 70% of its emergency applications since 2025.

The argued calendar runs late. CBS News lists religious schools and taxpayer-funded preschool on 3 November, semi-automatic rifle bans on 2 December, documentary proof of citizenship for voter registration on 8 December and third-country removals on 9 December. Approval sits at a record low, Gallup at 33% against 61% disapproval, and threats against federal judges reached 370 as of 1 July, up 31% on the year. Justice Elena Kagan, to a congressional panel: “When political figures try to intimidate judges … that’s where we really have crossed a line.”

Why it mattersA Court at half strength on its argued docket while an administration wins seven of ten unbriefed emergency applications has moved its centre of gravity off the calendar a practitioner can read.
