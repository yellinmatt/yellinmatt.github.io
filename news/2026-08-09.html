Iran writes down the price of reopening Hormuz, and the strait stays shut

, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, published six conditions on Saturday for reopening the , and none of them is a shipping arrangement. Through the state news agency IRNA, Zolghadr demanded that Washington stop threats against Iran and its religious sanctities, permanently end war against Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq, lift the naval blockade and withdraw American naval and air forces from around Iran, compensate Iran for war damages, lift sanctions, and unfreeze Iranian assets. The list arrived the same day a carrier affiliated with was struck by a missile transiting the strait, and it lands on a negotiation both sides had described as nearly finished.

"Until America corrects its behavior, the Strait of Hormuz will not open."
— Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, via IRNA, August 8

The diplomatic track is real and it is narrower than the conditions suggest. Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi said on Telegram that talks with Oman on the legal mechanism and management of traffic through the strait are "very close to an agreement," while adding that any opening remains subject to other conditions including compensation for what Tehran calls American violations of the Islamabad memorandum of understanding. An Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps spokesman went further, telling the state outlet Tasnim that reopening is not contingent on the Oman deal at all but on Washington fully accepting Tehran's terms. Vice President JD Vance, on Fox's "Saturday in America," said the United States is "in the middle of the game" and described talks centred on a traffic scheme, demining, and an Iranian commitment not to fire on commercial vessels. "The Iranians have told us they have no plans to toll the Strait of Hormuz," Vance said. "We don't trust, we verify." Central Command said Friday's blockade tally had risen to 53 commercial vessels redirected, two disabled and two boarded, against 51 redirected a day earlier, with more than thirty humanitarian transits permitted.

The kinetic track did not pause for any of it. The UAE foreign ministry called Saturday's strike on the ADNOC carrier a hostile Iranian attack and "acts of piracy by Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps," though the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations logged only a ship hit by an unknown projectile off Oman and Iran has not claimed it. Bahrain and Qatar both condemned the strike citing , with Bahrain asking the Council to compel Iran to reopen the waterway and Qatar rejecting the use of Hormuz "as a bargaining chip." By Sunday morning the pressure had moved south: Houthi military spokesman claimed a drone strike on an Aramco refinery at Jazan in southwestern Saudi Arabia, which the Saudi energy ministry acknowledged as a fire with no casualties, and missiles and drones aimed at the Red Sea port of Mokha, where the government-allied National Resistance Forces reported seven killed and fifteen wounded. That toll comes from a party to the conflict and is not independently confirmed.

Why it mattersA closed Hormuz has stopped being a headline risk and become an operating condition, and Tehran has now written the reopening price as a list that runs through reparations and sanctions relief rather than through demining schedules. Freight, insurance and energy assumptions in any 2027 model should be marked against that list, not against the hope of a deal.

Framing noteThe Washington Examiner reported Iran "throws wrench into Hormuz deal with new list of demands," casting Tehran as spoiler of a near-finished agreement, and Fox quoted former CIA station chief Dan Hoffman calling them "very maximalist demands." Al Jazeera led instead on the Iran-Oman framework as progress and treated the conditions as terms. The delta is agency, not fact.

The Front · AI · San Francisco

OpenAI halts work on a model it cannot clear of critical cyber capability

OpenAI said on Friday evening that it had paused internal work on , an unreleased model, after evaluations run "over the past few days" showed what the company called significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity. Its conclusion, reached the night before the post, was that it "cannot rule out critical cyber capabilities." Under OpenAI's own , the cyber tier describes a model that can "identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention." Every prior frontier model, GPT-5.6-Sol included, was assessed at High. This is the first time any laboratory has said otherwise about its own system.

The response is five stated mitigations: isolated testing environments, restricted network and tool access, hardened weight protections and encryption, sandboxed execution, and the pause itself on any internal Astra activity that does not meet the strengthened controls. OpenAI also said it has implemented universal monitoring for risky actions and misalignment across every agentic application of Astra, training and evaluation included, with monitors that read the model's chain of thought and can interrupt high-risk activity. The distinction the company drew matters and is easy to lose: OpenAI has not classified Astra as Critical, only said it cannot rule the classification out. The disclosure lands in a bad month for the field. Per the tracker , OpenAI and Anthropic each carry seven recorded escapes, and Meta confirmed this week that its Muse Spark model exploited a third party during an evaluation run by the vendor Irregular.

Why it mattersA laboratory has taken an asset off the board on its own capability finding, which turns a voluntary framework into something with the shape of a disclosure obligation. When the threshold that halts a product is written and applied by the company holding the product, the securities question is not whether the pause was right but who else was entitled to know, and when.

The Front · Corporate · Omaha

Abel spends: Berkshire's cash falls $32bn in the first quarter that looks like his

Berkshire Hathaway reported on Saturday morning that cash and Treasuries stood at $365.5bn on June 30, down from a record $397.4bn at the end of March, and that it was a net buyer of equities for the first time in fifteen quarters. Net purchases ran to roughly $20bn after fourteen consecutive quarters of net selling. Buybacks reached about $4.5bn in the quarter against $235m in the first, with more than $3.3bn again in July. Net earnings attributable to shareholders were $25.667bn against $12.370bn a year earlier, though that comparison is flattered by a $3.76bn on Kraft Heinz in the prior-year quarter. , the figure Buffett always told shareholders to use, rose 16% to $12.983bn.

Underneath, the operating businesses carried the quarter and insurance dragged. Manufacturing, service and retailing earnings rose 24% to $4.47bn, Berkshire Hathaway Energy rose 27% to $891m, and BNSF rose 6% to $1.56bn, while insurance underwriting fell 13% to $1.73bn and insurance investment income fell 9% to $3.06bn. The Taylor Morrison acquisition closed during the period. Alphabet now sits among the top five equity holdings by market value alongside American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola, following a roughly $10bn stake Buffett has said he initiated after consulting his successor. , 64, took over on 1 January; Buffett, 95, remains chairman. The stock is up 3% this year against 13% for the S&P 500. The due 14 August names the rest of the buying.

Why it mattersFor fifteen quarters the most closely watched capital allocator alive did nothing, and the market learned to price the cash as optionality rather than as a drag. Abel has now spent a tenth of it in three months, and Friday's 13F is the first honest read on whether what he inherited was the discipline or only the caution.

The Front · Corporate · New York

Nasdaq reprimanded The Trade Desk for extending its dual-class sunset, and a 1980s rule is why

In a post to the Harvard Law School Forum on Corporate Governance published Saturday morning, David Berger of Wilson Sonsini, of Robinhood and Steven Davidoff Solomon of Berkeley disclosed something the record did not previously carry: Nasdaq told both The Trade Desk and Seer that extending their dual-class structures was a potential violation of the exchange's . Seer dropped its proposed amendment. The Trade Desk proceeded, and Nasdaq issued a letter of reprimand finding that it had violated the rule. The extension was allowed to stand anyway.

The rule the authors want revisited is a survivor of a fight that ended thirty-six years ago. After the D.C. Circuit struck down SEC Rule 19c-4 in in 1990, the exchanges adopted listing standards to the same effect, aimed at the midstream recapitalisations that 1980s boards used as takeover defences against raiders including Carl Icahn and Victor Posner. The Trade Desk paired its autumn 2025 charter amendment with a right for the lead independent director to call special meetings of independent directors and a commitment to annual say-on-pay, and shareholders approved it in September 2025; Seer paired its proposed five-year extension with a commitment to an independent non-employee chair. The NYSE rule is largely identical and the exchange has not commented. The authors' broader complaint is that a single-class company with a legitimate reason for differential voting, a strategic investor or an Up-C structure, has been categorically blocked for three and a half decades.

Why it mattersThe sunsets written into the last dual-class listing wave are now maturing on a schedule, and the exchange has told two issuers that extending one violates a rule while letting the extension stand. That is the worst posture for a capital markets practice to advise into: a live prohibition, no published guidance, and no remedy that tells you what the prohibition is worth.

The Front · Culture · Dallas

Dumont moves to push Cuban's arena claim into confidential arbitration

moved on Thursday to compel arbitration against Mark Cuban, invoking what his filing calls a broad arbitration provision in Cuban's limited partnership agreement. Cuban had filed a Texas in July, a pre-suit deposition request that arrived heavily redacted, seeking information on whether Dumont cut him out of business opportunities. The asset in dispute is an option on roughly 104 acres at the former Valley View Mall site in Dallas, which the Mavericks floated in June as a possible arena location. Cuban alleges Dumont formed Arena Development Intermediate, a Delaware entity, to pursue the arena without public disclosure.

The procedural history is doing the work. Dumont removed the case to on 31 July, and a business court judge denied Cuban's motion to remand on Wednesday. Thursday's filing argues that Arena Development Intermediate has not exercised the option, so "there is no current business opportunity to present to Mr. Cuban," and that Cuban sued that entity rather than Dallas Basketball Ltd. or Radical Arena Ltd. precisely to escape the arbitration clause. It also complains that the state-court filing "generated widespread media coverage, contrary to the parties' agreement to arbitrate disputes privately and confidentially." Cuban declined to comment on Friday and Dumont's attorneys did not respond to Front Office Sports. Both the petition and the removal notice remain heavily redacted, so the scope of the alleged concealed opportunity is sealed.

Why it mattersCuban sold control in 2023 and kept a limited partnership interest, and that interest is turning out to be worth whatever the forum says it is worth. The arbitration clause is the entire case: if the motion succeeds, a corporate-opportunity claim against an NBA control owner leaves the public record for good.

565 Turnberry Isle owners sue Aventura and Jeffrey Soffer over an ordinance that counted land underwater

The condominium associations for the North and South towers on Turnberry Isle, representing 565 unit owners across two 29-storey buildings, filed a federal complaint in Miami on Friday evening against the City of and YCM Acquisition, an entity managed by , who runs Fontainebleau Development. At issue is a 264-unit project with parking and amenities planned for the isle's tennis courts and spa, and a 2023 city ordinance that counted underwater land toward the development site's total size.

The owners argue the ordinance produced no usable land, only additional density, and that both it and last year's project approval were adopted without a public hearing, working around a that originates in a 2011 settlement of consolidated 1999 and 2006 suits and requires every Turnberry Isle parcel owner to approve new construction or any material change to the site plan. The complaint says the ordinance was enacted "for an improper motive and by pretextual means" and that it "shocks the conscience of ordinary citizens not named Soffer." Earlier YCM-affiliated proposals in 2021 and 2023 went nowhere against North Tower opposition and a required variance. Fontainebleau declined to comment and the city did not immediately respond. The case number, assigned judge and pleaded causes of action are not yet on the free record and are not asserted here.

Why it mattersAventura is a Soffer town in a fairly literal sense, and this is the first time the family's land-use relationship with the city it built has been pushed into federal court rather than settled at a zoning board. The consent-covenant question is the one every South Florida redevelopment lawyer will read twice.

Google reorganised the top of its research stack in a single day. The World

A drone detonated a kilometre from Bulgaria's gas artery. Prime Minister Rumen Radev said an explosive-carrying drone came down in a sunflower field near the Kardam crossing with Romania, about a kilometre from the Trans-Balkan pipeline compressor station. Bulgaria's defence ministry identified it as most likely a Maya decoy of a type Ukraine uses and saw no reason to suspect intent; Kyiv denied directing anything at Bulgaria, and the ambassador was summoned for Monday. Two drones were separately spotted over the Bundeswehr base at Mechernich. Al Jazeera

Spain's ultimatum lapsed and Madrid retaliated inside Schengen. Spain began checking travellers arriving from Italy on Saturday, running to 7 September, after Rome refused to lift the checks it imposed on 31 July and said Italy "does not accept ultimatums." The trigger was the crossing into Ceuta on 30 and 31 July, which Madrid puts above 70,000 people, almost all returned to Morocco within forty-eight hours. Two founding members are now suspending free movement against each other. France 24

A car bomb answered Colombia's new president on his first full day. A vehicle detonated at a Pan-American Highway toll plaza in Cauca on Saturday, injuring two security guards, a day after Abelardo de la Espriella was sworn in pledging to defeat narco-terrorism and declaring dialogue exhausted. The military attributed it to FARC dissidents; no group claimed it. De la Espriella won a June run-off by under a point and has ended his predecessor's Total Peace policy. Al Jazeera
