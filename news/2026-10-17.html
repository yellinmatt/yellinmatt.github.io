Trump waives the Russia sanctions Congress passed, two days before they were due to bite

President Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving the “sanctions, restrictions, and duties” in the , which he signed last month after it cleared Congress with large bipartisan majorities, AP reported. Key provisions were due to start Oct. 18. The law authorized tariffs of up to 100 percent on imports from the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas, and restrictions on Russian banks, officials and companies that support the war. Trump certified to Congress that the waiver “is in the national interests of the United States,” without saying how.

“A craven, cowardly action.”
Sen. Richard Blumenthal, the bill’s co-author, October 16

The order holsters the law rather than repealing it. It formally establishes the tariffs and suspends their enforcement, Bloomberg reported, so they can be switched on without a new act. The waivers run until the president or the secretary of state, after consulting the Treasury and the trade representative, decides otherwise, and sanctions under earlier executive orders stand. It follows the Oct. 9 with Vladimir Putin.

Sen. Richard Blumenthal, a Democrat and the bill’s co-author, said the move had “everything to do with sucking up to Vladimir Putin.” Rep. Don Bacon, a Republican, said Trump was “prolonging the war, not ending it.” said the law “gives the president tools that he can use if he so chooses.” At the the same day, Zelenskyy put next year’s war cost at €155 billion and asked for at least €70 billion. The UN counted 328 civilians killed in September, up 85 percent on a year earlier.

Why it mattersSecondary tariffs that can be switched on by memo are a standing compliance risk for any deal touching the buyers of Russian energy. The statute now sits in reserve as leverage, and its timing tracks the diesel deal rather than the vote in Congress.

Framing noteThe right leads with authority and motive: the New York Post headline says the pause “is in national interest.” The left-leaning press leads with the cost: NBC says it “provides Russia a financial lifeline,” and CNN that the sanctions were shelved “before they even kick in.” The facts match; the emphasis does not.

Anthropic quietly pitches Congress a frontier-AI bill that would let only Commerce, through one court, halt a model

Anthropic representatives have privately shown lawmakers a draft frontier-AI safety bill, Politico reported on Friday, citing three people familiar with it. The government could block a model only for a foreseeable, imminent risk of more than $1 billion in damage, or of death or serious injury to more than 50 people, and only from three sources: a bioweapon, a model escaping its developer’s control, or a cyberattack.

Truist sells its $7.7 billion LightStream loan book to Carlyle

Truist agreed on Friday to sell substantially all of , its online unsecured-lending unit, to Carlyle: $7.7 billion of consumer loans for net proceeds of $7.2 billion, closing by year end, American Banker reported. Proceeds will repay wholesale borrowings, and Truist expects about $850 million, or 20 basis points, of added capital. Why it mattersBanks handing consumer credit to private funds is the private-credit trade in miniature, and Truist is one of Florida’s largest deposit franchises.
