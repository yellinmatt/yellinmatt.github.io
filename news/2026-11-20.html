Anthropic opens at $111.50 in its Nasdaq debut, then gives back half the gain to close up 17 percent at $97.17

Anthropic’s shares first traded at $111.50 at 11:48 a.m. on Thursday, about 34 percent above the $83 offering price, after a delayed , according to trade data compiled by StockTitan. The stock touched about $115 within minutes, fell to $92.72 by 1:18 p.m. and closed at $97.17, up 17.1 percent, on a day the broader market slid, NBC News and Fox News reported.

Voluntary, revocable expressions of each co-founder’s philanthropic intent.
Anthropic prospectus, as quoted by TechCrunch, Nov. 19

More than 830 million shares changed hands. On the 13.2 billion shares outstanding, the close values the company near $1.28 trillion; CNBC’s figure of $1.8 trillion appears to count stock. Each of the seven co-founders sold about 3.4 million shares, roughly $280 million apiece and $1.95 billion in all, TechCrunch reported, and each has pledged 80 percent of his or her equity to philanthropy, a promise the prospectus calls revocable.

Amazon’s 14.7 percent stake is worth more than $159 billion on $18 billion invested, TechCrunch said, and Google’s 13.7 percent is worth nearly $149 billion, and chip and cloud suppliers bought $26 billion in a placement at the offering price. Third-quarter revenue was $17 billion, against about $900 billion in computing commitments. Matt Levine titled Thursday’s column “Anthropic Pops.” CNBC reported the shares fell 6 percent in Friday premarket trading as Nvidia and AMD dropped.

Why it mattersA 17 percent first-day gain on an $81.3 billion raise leaves about $14 billion on the table, and the fade from the open is the number underwriters will defend when the next frontier lab files.

The Shenzhen declaration shields state officials from the ICC and pledges more refined fuel, and never mentions Ukraine

The White House on Thursday released a four-point declaration signed by Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin after about 90 minutes of talks in Shenzhen, Fox News and The Independent reported. It says “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought,” calls for reform of the United Nations, and commits the three to “increase production and supplies of .” A fourth point calls state consent and the of “paramount importance” and warns the ICC not to “abuse” its jurisdiction. The court holds warrants for Putin and Benjamin Netanyahu.

The text names no volumes, timetable or arms-control mechanism, and omits Ukraine, Europe and Iran. Trump rated the summit “a 12” out of 10, and Jared Kushner said Putin was “comfortable” with NATO-like guarantees for Ukraine, which would need Congress, Fox reported. Hours earlier, Russian strikes on Kyiv damaged the Austrian and Dutch embassies and killed one person, AFP reported. Trump landed at Joint Base Andrews at 8:01 p.m. and holds a press conference today.

Why it mattersThree nuclear powers, none an , put immunity for leaders into a joint text, a doctrinal move with more legal weight than its energy pledge.
