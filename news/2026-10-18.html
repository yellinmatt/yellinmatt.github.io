Trump waives the Graham Act before it bites, and the loudest objections come from Republicans

President Trump signed an executive order late Friday waiving implementation of the , the law he signed in September, before its penalties on Russia and on took effect. Spokeswoman Taylor Rogers said the president “wants to see this brutal war come to an end” and that enforcement now “would impede ongoing U.S. efforts to negotiate a durable and comprehensive peace agreement.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it a 60-day pause to analyse the economic impact, the Guardian reported.

The revolt came from the president’s side. “What part of enforcing a law passed 86-11 by the Senate does the President and his advisors not understand?” wrote Senator . John Cornyn: “Lindsey would be appalled.” Senator Darline Graham, who now holds the late senator’s seat, said she would keep the pressure on “because I don’t trust Putin.” Representative asked for a classified briefing, and Don Bacon said many members “won’t fund these things.” Thomas Massie praised the order.

“By giving the Russian economy life support, Trump’s deal with the devil keeps getting dirtier,” said Chuck Schumer; Richard Blumenthal said the waivers had “everything to do with sucking up to Vladimir Putin.” Volodymyr Zelensky said the waiver helps only Russia, then said Kyiv was ready to negotiate “in any format.” It arrives eight days after Trump’s diesel deal with Putin, which a bipartisan group already plans to undo when Congress returns after the election.

The President retains the ability to exercise these authorities at any time.
White House statement, October 17

Why it mattersA sanctions statute that the president can switch off by notice is a negotiating chip, not a compliance rule, so every Russia-exposed deal now prices a 60-day clock rather than a law.

AI borrowers’ loans start trading at 80 cents, and SoftBank’s default insurance costs half again what it did in January

The New York Times on Saturday put prices on last week’s selloff. Some bank debt for an Oracle data center in New Mexico, part of an $18 billion package, has traded around 80 cents on the dollar, and some for its Wisconsin site near 90. carries $72 billion of liabilities across 51 data centers, its bonds yield above 15 percent, and its stock fell 24 percent on the week; the Times wrote that markets imply a better than 60 percent chance it fails to meet its obligations. Insuring SoftBank’s debt now costs more than $40,000 a year per $1 million, against under $27,000 in January, through . Barclays measures the spread on AI debt up more than 50 percent in six months, and Axios reported that AI-related debt issuance has fallen sharply from June’s $113 billion and that JPMorgan is tightening.

Why it mattersWhen project loans trade at 80, the next AI financing is priced by distressed buyers, not relationship banks, and that is a different set of clients and documents.

News · Paris · October 17 · cross-spectrum LLLCLRR

About 550,000 march across France, and masked rioters burn Limoges’s town hall

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez put Saturday’s turnout at about 550,000 across 110 marches, 110,000 of them in Paris, CNN reported. The movement began nearly a month ago among students protesting school conditions after a summer heatwave and the cost of living, and unions, farmers and the joined. Masked protesters set fire to the ; city halls in Marseille and Lille were broken into. A protester in Saint-Étienne lost a hand, the second such injury since a ban on stun grenades was lifted. Nuñez put arrests at more than 1,800 on Sunday and ruled out a state of emergency. “No room for violence in our Republic,” said President Macron; Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu blamed “far-left groups,” and Marine Le Pen said rioters had “poisoned” legitimate grievances.

Why it mattersA government six months from a presidential vote now faces a street movement with a price tag, students want €10 billion for schools, and French fiscal space is already the bond market’s question.

Framing noteThe New York Times led on a “student movement” turning national; Fox News led on “far-left rioters” and a historic building ablaze.

Culture / EASL · St. Louis · October 16–17

Novig and Crypto.com sue Missouri as its deadline to stop selling sports contracts passes

Novig, the prediction market Sydney Sweeney has promoted, sued Missouri’s attorney general, Catherine Hanaway, and its gaming regulators on Friday in federal court in St. Louis, calling its contracts “quintessential swaps” under the Commodity Exchange Act. Crypto.com filed separately in the Western District, so the cases land before different judges. Hanaway’s September letters told the operators to stop or take a state license and pay its 10 percent tax by Saturday, the Post-Dispatch reported. Novig reported $125 million of volume in its first week of sports trading in August, Covers said, and became a in June. The courts are split: the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi, the Sixth and Ninth with the states, and the , which covers Missouri, has not ruled. The NFL has asked the Supreme Court to settle the question.

Why it mattersSports-betting regulation is now a federal preemption docket, and the client is a celebrity-marketed exchange whose ads are already a story.

Russia hits Ukraine’s grid with 332 drones, Ukraine sets St. Petersburg’s oil terminal alight, and Romanian F-16s down two

Russia launched 332 drones and ballistic missiles overnight into Friday at more than 15 power facilities, in what Zelensky called one of the largest strikes on Ukraine’s energy system; seven were killed and about 90 injured, the Kyiv Independent reported. Before dawn Sunday, Ukrainian drones set the and the Ruchyi depot on fire; the governor reported one dead and five hurt, and a VK data center was also struck. Leningrad region officials said 127 drones were downed. On Saturday two Romanian F-16s shot down two jet-powered drones over Romanian waters near .

Why it mattersEach side is now hitting the other’s fuel and power on purpose, and a NATO member firing inside its own waters is the escalation no ceasefire draft has priced.

Law · Washington · October 16 · cross-spectrum LLLLRR

The Justice Department moves to disqualify Judge Ana Reyes in the East Potomac trees case, a day before her hearing

Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward signed a motion filed at 8:33 p.m. Friday asking Judge Ana Reyes to step aside under , Golf Channel reported. It calls her conduct “nothing short of egregious” and alleges “deep-seated and unequivocal antipathy toward the Trump Administration,” citing her remarks in the transgender-troops case. The government had given notice at 4:55 p.m. on Friday, October 9, of removing up to 529 trees, and crews started the next morning. Reyes halted the work at the with a 14-day restraining order after several hundred came down; counts vary by outlet. The motion also asks her to release the audio of Monday’s hearing. That hearing, with senior officials ordered to testify, is set for 10 a.m.

Why it mattersA recusal motion signed by the department’s third-ranking official turns a tree dispute into a test of whether leadership can pick its judge by attacking one.
