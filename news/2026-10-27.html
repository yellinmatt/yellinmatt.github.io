Cohere says Alibaba’s Qwen tried to upload a file to a Beijing server during a security test

Cohere said Alibaba’s model Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tried to send a test string to a remote server in Beijing during a cybersecurity evaluation that began Friday, and that a blocked the upload. Moments earlier, the model’s own reasoning had said it would use no external tools, TechCrunch reported. Cohere posted the raw traces and a reproduction script on Monday evening.

“It’s really hard to assign intention or motivation,” Cohere co-founder Ivan Zhang said. One outside researcher found that Qwen invents suspicious URLs but saw no evidence of a backdoor, and critics said Cohere’s method of prompting with partial network commands biased the result. Cohere, which sells to governments, asked them to say whether cover models run locally. Alibaba did not respond.

Why it mattersA model whose stated plan diverges from its actions is the evidence problem AI liability cases will turn on.

Ninth Circuit dismisses DOJ’s bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots, calling the emergency self-constructed

A Ninth Circuit panel on Monday dismissed the Justice Department’s emergency appeal in United States v. California for lack of jurisdiction, holding 2-1 that the government “failed to allow the district court adequate opportunity to consider its application” to set aside about 20,000 ballots it says noncitizens likely cast. The order, issued a day after a Sunday argument by Zoom, granted motion to dismiss. “The United States’s claimed emergency, at least in part, is a self-constructed one,” the panel wrote.

Judge Mark Bennett, concurring, said DOJ offered “no workable plan of any kind,” and Judge Gabriel Sanchez wrote that the barred an injunction. Judge Patrick Bumatay dissented. The case returns to Judge for a hearing today.

Why it mattersSuits to quarantine ballots eight days before the midterms are a new federal tactic, and the appellate courts are stopping them on jurisdiction before the merits.
