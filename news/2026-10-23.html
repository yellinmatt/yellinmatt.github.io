Meta creates a nonvoting share class as its AI spending pushes free cash flow below zero

Meta will pay every holder one new nonvoting for each Class A and Class B share, in effect a two-for-one split, once an amended charter takes effect on Jan. 19, 2027, the company said Wednesday. The terms limit him. Only 20% of his new shares are freely saleable; for each of the rest he sells, he must sell or convert a matching voting share within two business days, and 2.5% are released each quarter over eight years. Earnings of $3.83 a share include a $9.89 billion charge for the August settlement with 52 state attorneys general. Meta fell 6.98% to $648.85 on Thursday as chip stocks sold off after flagged supply limits; the Nasdaq lost about 2.6% and the about 6.7%.
