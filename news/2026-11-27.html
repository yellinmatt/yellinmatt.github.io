Altria weighs an all-cash bid for Imperial Brands, with Japan Tobacco in talks to take a share of the business

Altria, the maker of Marlboro in the United States, said on Thursday that it is considering a possible all-cash offer for and is in talks with another party about buying part of the business, Reuters reported. No agreement has been reached. Imperial said it had received no approach but would consider any proposal. Its shares closed 13.3 percent higher in London, giving it a market value of £25.90 billion ($34 billion). Under the UK , Altria has until 5 p.m. London time on Dec. 24 to make a firm offer or withdraw, unless the deadline is extended.

… turn JT into a sizeable, diversified, and value-rational 3rd-place US competitor with cost synergy potential.
Andrei Andon-Ionita, Jefferies analyst, on a deal, via Reuters, Nov. 27

Bloomberg reported that is weighing a joint bid. Under one structure reported by the Financial Times, Altria would take Imperial’s international operations and JT its US business. JT shares closed up 4.5 percent in Tokyo on Friday, against a 1.7 percent rise in the Topix. JT declined to comment.

Neither buyer holds much cash. JT had 887.3 billion yen ($5.6 billion) at the end of September and Altria $2.7 billion, Reuters reported. BTIG’s Owen Bennett said JT could reissue about $7.4 billion of treasury shares. Morningstar’s Kristoffer Inton warned the deal could push Altria to its highest net leverage on record. Altria has sold only in the United States since the 2008 spinoff of . At a premium, the deal would be the third-largest tobacco takeover on record, according to LSEG data, and the largest since BAT bought for $60 billion, including debt, in 2016. No advisers have been named.

Why it mattersA cash bid that neither buyer can fund from its balance sheet means a large acquisition financing and probably a split of the target along regulatory lines. The structure, the leverage and the UK clock will all be set by Christmas Eve.

BTIG said a deal could be done without hurting either buyer’s credit rating, while Morningstar said Altria might need several years to return to its leverage target even with buybacks paused. Reuters analysts said a sensible split would give JT the US assets and Altria some or all of the international business.

What’s next. Under the Takeover Code, Altria must announce a firm intention to bid or withdraw by 5 p.m. London time on Dec. 24, unless the Takeover Panel grants an extension.

The World · Kuwait City · cross-spectrum: L LL C R

A drone strike on a Kuwaiti air-defense site kills two Italian Rheinmetall technicians, and Iran calls it a false flag

Two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia were killed on Thursday when drones struck Kuwaiti military sites, including an air-defense station Kuwait’s defense ministry called a “vital site,” AFP reported. Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, were servicing antennas, Fox News reported. Kuwait said they were killed by shrapnel from intercepted Iranian drones. AFP and Fox reported two others wounded; CBS put the injured at four.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the strike “suspicious,” said it “looks like a pre-cooked recipe for war” and offered a joint investigation. A military spokesman blamed a US and Israeli false flag, while outlets close to the Revolutionary Guards blamed Ukraine. Gulf states condemned the attack, and Bahrain asked the Security Council for accountability. “We must step up pressure on Iran,” said Ursula von der Leyen. Italy had moved most of its troops out of Kuwait in October.

Why it mattersThese are the first European civilian deaths in the Gulf strikes, and the dead were defense contractors. Every Western supplier with staff at Gulf bases now carries that risk on its books.

In a Thanksgiving call with US troops on Thursday, Trump said “we have victory at hand,” Al Jazeera reported. Accounts of the wounded differ between outlets.

Euro-area leaders choose Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Lagarde at the European Central Bank

Leaders of the 21 euro-area countries on Thursday chose Pablo Hernández de Cos, the former Bank of Spain governor, to succeed Christine Lagarde as president of the European Central Bank, AP reported. He beat Klaas Knot, seen as a hawk. Hernández de Cos sat on the ECB’s Governing Council from 2018 to 2024 and now runs the . Commerzbank calls him a dove; in 2020 he backed the €1.85 trillion pandemic bond-buying program.

Lagarde said on Oct. 30 that she would step down early, effective Feb. 7, 2027, Euronews reported. AP said the timing lets leaders choose while Emmanuel Macron is still in office. The package also names Princeton’s Markus Brunnermeier to replace Philip Lane in May and Laurence Boone to take Isabel Schnabel’s seat in January. The choice still needs consultation with the European Parliament, which AP called a formality. Euro-area inflation is 4 percent, double the target, and the ECB meets next on Dec. 17.

Why it mattersA dovish president from the south takes over with inflation at twice the target. Every euro sovereign spread, and every bank capital plan priced off them, now carries that bet.

Source: AP (via ABC News) ↗ · Also reported by: The Guardian · Euronews · South China Morning Post

Background. Lagarde announced her early exit on Oct. 30. AP reported that choosing now lets leaders settle the post before France’s next presidential election. Euro-area inflation stands at 4 percent, partly because of the Iran war, and AP said a rate increase remains possible. Hernández de Cos left the Bank of Spain in 2024 for the BIS.

What’s next. The ECB’s Governing Council meets on Dec. 17. The appointment still needs consultation with the European Parliament and an opinion from the council.

Nvidia tells Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping its servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers built on its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, with no end date, TechCrunch reported on Thursday, following reports by the Financial Times and the Wall Street Journal. Dell and Supermicro told buyers Nvidia would not authorize the orders. None of the three companies commented.

The halt follows a Commerce Department rule on to advanced chips, which took effect on Nov. 19. The estimates offshore data centers supply about 24 percent of China’s AI compute. Nvidia’s Singapore unit booked $104 billion of revenue in 2025, up 72 percent, the FT reported. Lenders had already paused a $480 million GPU loan for a Malaysian site that ByteDance would use, Bloomberg reported on Nov. 17.

Why it mattersThe rule reaches the debt as well as the chips. Every GPU-backed loan in Southeast Asia now turns on who rents the compute, and lenders must underwrite the tenant list.

The halt follows Nvidia’s board reinstating Jensen Huang earlier this week.

Law & the Courts · London

Undisclosed AI use touched at least 25 court cases in England and Wales this year, an investigation finds

A review of published judgments by the Bureau of Investigative Journalism and the Guardian found at least 25 cases in England and Wales this year affected by undisclosed AI, including AI-written witness statements and invented case law. A barrister in a £16 million High Court dispute between two Nigerian oil companies relied on a ruling that does not exist. A police officer used Copilot to draft a victim’s statement. The wants a declaration rule. Lord Sales warned that the deeper risk is losing the skill to spot when AI is wrong.

Why it mattersFake citations are now a pattern across a whole court system, not a stray sanction. A signed declaration would make verification a duty every litigator carries.

Source: The Bureau of Investigative Journalism ↗ · Also reported by: The Guardian (via AOL)
Go deeper
  • At least 25 cases in England and Wales were affected by undisclosed AI this year.
  • A barrister relied on an invented ruling in a £16 million dispute.
  • The Civil Justice Council proposes a signed declaration for witnesses of fact.

Background. The Court of Appeal criticized the police officer’s use of Copilot, and the officer was removed from frontline duty, the Bureau reported. An insolvency judge found a junior solicitor had cited a statute that does not exist, and a Crown Prosecution Service lawyer cited an imaginary precedent earlier this month. The council’s working group is led by Sir Colin Birss.

Law & the Courts · Washington · cross-spectrum: LL C

A judge declines a long-term ban on taxpayer-funded pro-Trump ads because the midterms are over

Judge Loren AliKhan on Wednesday declined to extend her order blocking the administration from distributing at least 11 government ads the Democratic National Committee calls propaganda, paid for partly with $20 million in Homeland Security contract funds, AP reported on Thursday. She found the DNC likely to win on the merits but denied relief because “the November 2026 midterm elections have come and gone.” Accepting that campaigns are permanent, she wrote, would let the DNC seek “in perpetuity.” The DNC said it “will fight on.”

Why it mattersA plaintiff can be right on the law and still lose on timing. Election-season relief expires with the election.

Three states block a €45 billion EU loan for Ukraine, and leaders push the seven-year budget to December

An extraordinary EU summit in Brussels ended early on Friday without agreement on the bloc’s 2028 to 2034 budget or a €45 billion loan for Ukraine’s 2027 needs, Al Jazeera reported. “24 member states supported the proposal,” said Council President António Costa. “We will return to the issue at the summit in December.” Hungary, Slovakia and the Czech Republic oppose the loan, which would come on top of the €90 billion agreed last December and be repaid only once Ukraine receives .

Costa’s compromise budget of about €1.78 trillion is roughly 10 percent below the Commission’s proposal, Euronews reported, and needs . “We can’t and we mustn’t take on more debt,” said Friedrich Merz. Emmanuel Macron said a deal by Dec. 31 is “the absolute limit.” Hungary’s prime minister, Péter Magyar, said the chance of agreement was “virtually zero.” Seven net contributors, among them Germany, the Netherlands and Sweden, boycotted Costa’s small-group format.

Why it mattersUkraine’s 2027 financing now depends on three governments and the EU’s borrowing capacity. Any EU bond issued to fund it will price that risk.

Argentina bars its central bank from printing money to fund the government

Argentina’s Chamber of Deputies on Thursday gave final approval, 141 to 0 with 99 abstentions, to a law that defines preserving the peso’s value as the central bank’s “primary and fundamental mission” and restricts of the Treasury, Al Jazeera reported. The Senate stripped Javier Milei’s proposed two-thirds supermajority to remove the bank’s governors. The lower house first passed the bill 144 to 102 in August. Why it mattersA statutory ban on monetary financing is what sovereign bondholders have asked of Buenos Aires for decades. The weaker removal clause is the gap they will price.

Rockstar’s UK developers vote about three to one against recognizing a union in GTA VI’s launch week

About 75 percent of Rockstar’s UK staff voted against a recognition agreement with the Game Workers Union in a ballot that ran from Nov. 12 to Nov. 26, Kotaku reported. The deal would have covered bargaining on overtime, bonuses, layoffs and AI. Jennifer Kolbe, Rockstar’s head of publishing, said the result reflects support for “direct relationships.” Rockstar fired 34 workers active in organizing in 2025; their claim awaits judgment. Turnout was not reported.

Why it mattersThe biggest launch in games history arrived with its workforce declining collective bargaining. The tribunal ruling now carries the labor question.
