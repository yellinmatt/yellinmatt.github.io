AI models find a 14-year-old flaw in PHP, and governments pull websites offline to patch it

The PHP Foundation issued an emergency patch on Wednesday for two critical flaws in , the server-side language behind much of the web, after AI models found them, Fortune reported. The bugs, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 and together called php2shell, let an attacker and reach the network behind it. The vulnerable code had sat in PHP for about 14 years.

, a firm in OpenAI’s Patch the Planet program, found the first flaw on Aug. 1 using GPT-5.6 Sol, and found it independently with Google and OpenAI models. OpenAI then ran a with PHP’s maintainers, Cloudflare, CISA and the White House’s Super Intelligence Force, and committed $1 billion in subsidized access to its Daybreak security tools for under-resourced defenders. It called the bug “one of the most impactful security issues in recent memory.” Greg Brockman said a limited version was found in 2024 and dismissed.

“It marks an AI inflection point.”
Wiz, company blog, October 21

Palo Alto Networks counts 22.6 million potentially vulnerable instances; Wiz’s claim that 70% of the internet is affected has not been verified. The NSA and FBI urged immediate updates, and agencies in the Netherlands and Finland took sites offline as a precaution, Reuters reported. Japan’s prime minister and the European Union’s CERT also urged updates. Cloudflare’s chief technology officer said early attacks had begun. Discovery dates differ by outlet, and confirmed breaches remain limited.

Why it mattersThe model that finds a flaw can also write the exploit, so patch windows shrink from months to days. That resets what counts as reasonable security in breach suits and disclosure once an old bug becomes cheap to find.
