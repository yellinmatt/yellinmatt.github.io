About

Matthew Yellin

I publish something every morning. A brief of the day's news worth knowing, one lesson toward a general education, and an archive of the stand-up worth keeping, all written from Miami and all finished before most people are awake.

I started because I wanted the discipline more than I wanted the output. Publishing on a fixed schedule removes the option of waiting until you feel ready, and a thing that has to go out at six is a thing you actually finish. The archive turned out to be a side effect of the habit rather than the point of it.

The other reason is that explaining something is the only honest test of whether you understood it. It is easy to read a page about how a bond works and come away with a warm feeling of comprehension that evaporates the moment somebody asks a question. Writing the explanation down, for a reader who will not accept hand-waving, is where the gaps turn up, and nearly everything here exists because I wanted to find my own.

So the work splits in two. Some of it is published, written for anyone who wants to read it, and it arrives on a schedule. The rest is tools, built for an audience of one, because the thing I wanted did not exist and it was quicker to make it than to keep wishing for it. Those are not for sale and mostly not for sharing, but they are the honest half of what I do all day, so they are listed too.
