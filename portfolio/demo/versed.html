Lesson No. 142 · animal behaviour

The alpha wolf does not exist

The most repeated fact about wolves came from watching the wrong wolves, in the wrong place, doing the wrong thing.

The study everyone cites put unrelated captive wolves in an enclosure and watched them fight for rank. That is not a pack. In the wild a pack is a family: two parents and their young of several years, and the animal doing the "dominating" is a father.

A Yellowstone pack, 2019. The two adults at the front are the breeding pair, which is the entire hierarchy.

The man who ran that study, David Mech, spent the next thirty years trying to withdraw the word. His own publisher kept the book in print over his objection, which is most of why the idea outlived the evidence.

And now the correction gets corrected. "It's just a family" is also too tidy. Large packs do form from several litters, dispersal creates unrelated neighbours, and food scarcity produces exactly the tension the captive studies described. The captive work was not wrong about behaviour; it was wrong about which situation it described.
