The Old Fashioned

Why is it called an Old Fashioned? Who invented it? And what actually goes in one? The answers start in 1806, with one candidate’s bar tab.

The bar bill

A candidate loses an election. His bar tab makes history.

American candidates used to buy the voters drinks. The tag marks one voter getting his free drink.

May 1806, Hudson, New York. A candidate loses, and the town paper prints his bar tab as a joke.

  • 720rum grogs
  • 411glasses of bitters
  • 32gin slings
  • 17brandies
  • 25cock-tails

Nothing

That was his gain. Now look at the last drink on the tab.

“I have heard of a jorum, of phlegm-cutter and fog driver … but never in my life … did I hear of cock tail before.”

A Subscriber

A reader knew every drink on the tab but one. He asked the editor what a cock-tail was.

The editor answered with a recipe of four ingredients. Pick the four.

  • Spirits
  • Ice
  • Lemon juice
  • Sugar
  • An egg
  • Wine
  • Mint
  • Water
  • Honey
  • Soda water
  • Bitters
  • A cherry

Spirits (any strong liquor), sugar, water and bitters. It had no ice, fruit or juice.

“Cock tail, then, is a stimulating liquor, composed of spirits of any kind, sugar, water, and bitters.”

The oldest definition of a cocktail ever found

Nobody knows who mixed the first one. This is the first time anyone wrote down what was in it.

The editor

Harry Croswell

27 years old in 1806. Pictured in old age.

  • His paperThe Balance, in Hudson, New York.
  • His habitAttacking politicians. One attack on President Jefferson got him convicted of libel.
  • Why he mattersHis answer to one confused reader is the oldest cocktail recipe on record.
“… a person having swallowed a glass of it, is ready to swallow any thing else.”

“Swallow” also means “believe”. Croswell’s joke: a voter full of cocktails will believe any candidate.

The oldest cocktail recipe is the setup for a political joke.

Sugar, bitters, a splash of water, whiskey. Two things here are newer than 1806: the ice and the name.

Spirits, sugar, water and bitters. In print since 1806.

The ice

A 22-year-old decides to sell ice to the tropics.

In 1806 ice came from ponds. Each winter, workers sawed the frozen surface into blocks and stored them under sawdust.

The same work, a century later and still done by hand: measure, saw, float the blocks, haul them to the icehouse.

The merchant

Frederic Tudor

22 years old in 1806. Pictured in old age.

  • Who he wasA rich Boston family’s son, with a pond on the family land.
  • His ideaCut pond ice in winter. Sell it where water never freezes.
  • Why he mattersHe started the trade that put ice in American drinks.

February 1806. He ships about 130 tons of pond ice to Martinique, in the Caribbean. His hometown paper’s report began: “No joke.”

The ice arrived, but Martinique had no icehouse. He sold it off the ship as it melted. Later his debts put him in prison.

He kept at it. In 1833 his ice left Boston for Calcutta: 16,000 miles, four months, two crossings of the equator.

180 tons went into the hold. How much was still ice in India?

About 100 tons, kept frozen under sawdust. Within three days Calcutta was planning an icehouse.

He also had to make people want ice. His salesmen gave it to bartenders free and taught them to make cold drinks.

By the 1850s Boston shipped that much each year. Tudor was now called the Ice King.

It chills the drink. As it melts, it adds the water.

The bitters

The most famous bottle of bitters began as army medicine.

Bitters are strong alcohol soaked with bitter herbs and spices. A few dashes season a drink the way salt seasons food.

The doctor

Johann Siegert

A German army doctor

  • Where he wentVenezuela, 1820, as chief surgeon to Simón Bolívar’s army in its war against Spain.
  • What he madeA tonic for soldiers’ stomach trouble, finished in 1824 in the town of Angostura.
  • Why he mattersThat tonic is the bitters in an Old Fashioned today.

A bitter tree bark is also called angostura. Is it in the bottle?

  • Yes
  • No

No. The bitters are named for the town, now called Ciudad Bolívar.

The cocktail had its ice and its bitters. No bartender had yet written a guide to making one.

Bitters season the drink. The best-known brand began as an army doctor’s tonic.

The barman

One bartender writes it all down.

A saloon scene staged for an early Edison film. The man in white behind the counter is the barman.

Jerry Thomas’s best-known trick was the Blue Blazer: whiskey and boiling water, set alight and poured from mug to mug.

The barman

Jerry Thomas

Born in 1830 in Sackets Harbor, New York

  • Before the barAt 18 he sailed to California for the gold rush.
  • By 1858He ran the bar at New York’s Metropolitan Hotel, the best-known bartender in America.
  • Why he mattersIn 1862 he published the first bartender’s guide.

The first bartender’s guide ever published

How to Mix Drinks, published in New York in 1862.

“The ‘Cocktail’ is a modern invention … some patients insist that it is good in the morning as a tonic.”

In 1862 some people drank cocktails at breakfast, as medicine.

“3 or 4 dashes of gum syrup. 2 do. bitters. 1 wine-glass of whiskey, and a piece of lemon peel. Fill one-third full of fine ice.”

Sugar, bitters and spirit, as in 1806. The spirit is now whiskey, and there is ice.

Thomas did not die rich.

  • 1872Opens a Broadway bar hung with portraits of himself.
  • 1885Dies in New York, his money lost on the stock market.

After him, bartenders began adding liqueurs to the cocktail.

In 1862 the cocktail entered the first bartender’s guide, with whiskey and ice.

The name

Bartenders dress the cocktail up. Some drinkers want it back.

By the 1880s the best American bars looked like palaces, and their bartenders made more elaborate drinks.

Bartenders “improved” the whiskey cocktail with three additions. Pick the three.

  • Egg white
  • Cherry liqueur
  • Soda water
  • Absinthe
  • Mint
  • Pineapple syrup
  • Orange liqueur
  • Red wine
  • Honey

Cherry liqueur, absinthe and orange liqueur. Absinthe is a strong green spirit that tastes of anise.

“2 dashes Maraschino. 1 dash Absinthe.”

Two lines from the “Improved” recipes that Jerry Thomas’s guide added in 1876. Maraschino is the cherry liqueur.

Some drinkers hated the extras. They asked for their cocktail the old-fashioned way.

The Chicago Tribune mentions “old-fashioned cocktails”. It is the name’s first known appearance in print.

“Dissolve a small lump of sugar with a little water … add two dashes Angostura bitters, a small piece ice, a piece lemon-peel, one jigger whiskey.”

By 1895 the order had its own recipe. Count the highlights: the four from 1806, plus ice and peel.

That is why it is called an Old Fashioned. The name was the order for a cocktail made the old way.

“Old-fashioned” was how drinkers ordered the original cocktail.

The myth

A Kentucky club says it invented the drink.

Many people say the drink was invented here, at the Pendennis Club in Louisville, for a bourbon maker named Colonel James Pepper.

The story says Pepper carried the recipe to the Waldorf in New York. It comes from a 1931 book about that bar.

The name was in print by 1880. Guess the year the Pendennis Club was founded.

1881. The name is a year older than the club. The recipe is 75 years older.

Line up the dates.

  • 1806The recipe is printed in Hudson.
  • 1862Jerry Thomas publishes it, with ice.
  • 1880The name appears in the Chicago Tribune.
  • 1881The Pendennis Club is founded.
  • 1931The Pendennis story appears in a book.

The club could not have invented a drink that already had its name.

The drink and its name are both older than the club.

The comeback

The drink gets banned, then ruined, then rescued.

The 1901 saloon film again, a minute later. In 1900 Carrie Nation had begun smashing saloons to protest alcohol, and Edison made a comedy of it.

In 1920 the United States banned alcohol. Prohibition lasted until 1933. Here, agents pour a barrel into a New York sewer.

Left: the 1940s version, with orange and cherry mashed in. Some say the fruit first hid bad Prohibition liquor. Right: the drink today.

In the late 1990s, bartenders went back to old guides like Jerry Thomas’s and took the fruit out.

In a yearly survey of 100 top bars, it was the best-selling classic cocktail seven years running. The Negroni passed it in 2022.

Bartenders took the mashed fruit back out in the 1990s.

Your turn

You have every piece. Now build the drink.

Build one. Tap the ingredients in the order they go in.

  1. Sugar cubePut one sugar cube in the glass.
  2. BittersShake a few dashes of bitters onto the cube.
  3. WaterAdd a few dashes of water. Crush and stir until the sugar dissolves.
  4. IceFill the glass with ice.
  5. WhiskeyPour in 45 ml of bourbon or rye. Stir gently.
  6. Orange peelFinish with a strip of orange peel.

That is the International Bartenders Association recipe: 1806, plus ice and a strip of peel.

An Old Fashioned is spirit, sugar and bitters over ice. Which of these is one?

  • Tequila, orange liqueur, lime juice
  • Rye whiskey, sweet vermouth, bitters
  • Tequila, mezcal, agave syrup, bitters

The third, an Oaxaca Old Fashioned, sweetened with agave. A Margarita adds lime juice. A Manhattan swaps the sugar for vermouth.

Three common questions.

  • Bourbon or rye?Either. Bourbon is sweeter, rye is drier.
  • Sugar cube or syrup?Either. Syrup mixes faster.
  • Fruit?A strip of orange peel. No mashed fruit.

The Old Fashioned is the oldest cocktail recipe on record. It got its name when drinkers had to ask for it the old way.
